# Simulating Green Beard Altruism (Primer, 13:23)

## Arc
Hook question (why be nice if evolution rewards the fittest?), then a baseline blob ecosystem with no altruism (0:16-1:38). Add one choice, coward or altruist (1:38). Altruism dies twice (3:21-4:12). Many runs (4:12). A post-mortem tally shows why (5:00). Invent a visible tag, the green beard (6:07). It wins (6:38). Stress it: harsher cost, low starting frequency (7:29-8:51). Split the bundled gene into four blob types and it collapses (9:22-11:53). Honest downer, teaser, sponsor.

## Strongest moves
1. **Predict-then-reveal, repeated** (4:29, 6:38, 7:29, 8:01, 10:43). At 4:29 he says why: brains pretend they knew it all along. Five pauses, each before a graph. Learner discovers the result through visible repetition.
2. **Audit the win** (5:00-5:51). Exact counts show altruists saved a net 61,630 copies, yet still went extinct because population is capped. This replaces the naive metric (total saved) with the right one (relative to competitors) using a concrete tally.
3. **Failure motivates design** (3:21 to 6:22). Altruism fails at 50% cost, then at 10% cost, then across 30 runs. Only then does he ask how altruists could favour themselves, and the green beard is a you-could-invent-it step.
4. **Planted caveat, later callback** (2:25 and 9:22). Early honest-caveat ("real behaviour is many genes"). At 9:22 he reuses it, noting one allele does too much, and splits it into two genes. The caveat becomes the next stepping stone.
5. **Naming the four types** (9:54-10:25). Colour plus a character name (suckers, impostors, true beards, cowards) makes the final graph readable by story ("suckers help but never get help").

## Unusual
- Notation (allele-frequency graph, thin vs thick lines) is explained after you have watched a sim, never before.
- Drops individual run lines when they become noise (11:04) rather than defending the chart.
- Ends on a downer instead of a triumph.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11. Partly: 3, 4 (no real analogy, rigor via tallies), 8 (speech stays steady; pacing comes from long silent sim stretches and pause-prompts), 12, 13. Not contradicted anywhere.

## Velocity implications
- Make batch-run scenes (many runs plus average) and a hard predict-first pause standard.
- Learner model should store predictions and compare with outcomes.
- Voice should plant caveats early and call them back when complexity is added.
- Add an eval: every new mechanic must be motivated by a failure the learner just watched.
- Wins deserve an audit scene that asks whether the metric is right.
