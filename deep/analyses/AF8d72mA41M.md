# Blue LED (Veritasium, 34 min) teardown

## Arc
Misconception hook (LED color is not the plastic), history of the missing blue, then protagonist Nakamura. A 5-minute physics block (4:06-9:36) builds from stadium seats to bands, doping, p-n junction, band gap and color, and ends on why blue is hard. The back half follows Nakamura through three hurdles (crystal quality, p-type, brightness), with Akasaki and Amano as rivals. Aftermath: lawsuit, world impact, Nobel, callback to his PhD backup plan.

## Strongest moves
1. **Story creates the need for physics (1:38 -> 4:06).** Stakes (a 3M yen gamble) come before the band theory, so the lecture is the answer to "why can't anyone make blue".
2. **Stadium-seat analogy (4:06-6:02).** Energy levels are seats, valence band is the floor, conduction band the balcony, holes are empty seats moving. One image covers conductors, insulators and semiconductors.
3. **Define terms by their use (6:18-7:41).** n-type and p-type are explained as 'mobile carriers negative or positive', with an explicit note that the material is still neutral. This is a minimal-definition-then-refine move that avoids a common misreading.
4. **Hurdle-triple pattern (9:54, 12:54, 21:57).** The same beat structure repeats: obstacle, others failed or partly solved, Nakamura's simple or brute-force trick (annealing instead of electron beam, 22:46 pumping indium). Repetition lets viewers anticipate.
5. **Callback close (31:35).** The PhD backup plan from 11:50 resolves, and the conclusion generalises to determination.

## Unusual
Very long; narrative drives pace. Nearly no equations. Physics is front-loaded in a single block instead of dripped through the story. Interview clips with Nakamura are used as evidence, not as teaching.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 4, 9, 11, 12. Partly: 5, 6, 7, 10, 13. Not observed: 3. Principle 8: speech stays brisk; slowing happens through analogy time (3 minutes on seats before any term like band gap) and through story digressions.

## Velocity implications
- Make the learner care first: orchestrator should surface a concrete obstacle before opening theory.
- Keep one analogy alive across several concepts and name where it stops.
- Define a term as "what it means for what you can observe" and add the pitfall (the material stays neutral).
- A hurdle roadmap in the learner model lets Velocity reuse a repeated pattern across sub-problems.
- Scene builder: seat-based band diagram with moving electrons and holes.
