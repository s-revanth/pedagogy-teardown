# Simulating the Evolution of Aggression (Primer, 13 min)

## Arc
Reused world (food, survive, reproduce) with one twist, food in pairs. One strategy (Dove) runs alone, then Hawk is added, and the learner is asked to predict the mix (2:25). The sim reveals a roughly half-and-half fluctuating population. Only after that does a payoff table appear (4:36), then arrows, then Nash equilibrium (named at 6:41), then a guessed 90% case (7:55), variables (9:24), a crossing-lines graph (9:44), a big-sim check (10:05), and a parameter dial that ends at the prisoner's dilemma (12:09).

## Strongest moves
1. **Sim before notation (0:42 to 4:36).** The learner watches the equilibrium emerge for almost four minutes before any table. The table then reads as a compression of rules they have already seen in action.
2. **Dignify-then-dismiss the human intuition (5:28, 6:03).** The narrator raises the clever human reply (retaliate, reward kindness) and explains why these forgetful, one-meeting blobs cannot use it. This pre-empts the learner's likely objection and plants a teaser for repeated games.
3. **Brute force before variables (7:55 to 9:24).** The 90% dove case is computed by hand (0.95), then the learner is told to pause and compute the hawk score (1.35). Only then do letters arrive, with an apology ("a lot of letters") and an immediate removal of one via h = 1 - d.
4. **Honest redundancy (10:05).** It admits the math only confirmed what the sim showed, then pays it off at 10:21: change one payoff and the equilibrium moves to 1/3 doves, predicted and then confirmed.
5. **Parameter-dial ending (12:09).** Turning hawk-vs-hawk up to 3/4 makes the lines stop crossing and the arrows flip, so the model breaks into a named classic. This works as a bridge to the next video.

## Unusual
No verbal analogy at all; the blobs are the concrete world. The viewer is cast as a blob controller (5:10). Definitions are minimal at first ("equilibrium = nobody gains by changing"), then refined to equal expected scores (7:21). The one non-flat beat is the 1:34 to 2:10 rule exposition, which is a dense rule list delivered once.

## Velocity implications
- Orchestrator: run the sim first, notation second; use the formal layer to explain what was seen, then to predict a changed run.
- Scene builder: payoff grid with a live dial on one cell, linked to the crossing-lines graph and the sim.
- Voice: when the learner proposes a human strategy, acknowledge it and name the model assumption that rules it out.
- Ghost hands: write the first case in full, fill symmetric cases fast ("same reasoning").
- Evals: check a predict prompt precedes each sim reveal.
