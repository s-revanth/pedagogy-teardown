# OverSimplified: The American Revolution, Part 1 (15 min)

## Arc
Cold open with Columbus complaining that he did not reach India (0:00), a pre-emptive Vikings caveat (0:47), sponsor (1:04-1:35), then the Seven Years' War debt (1:51-2:57), the tax and boycott cycle (2:57-4:53), Boston Massacre and Tea Party (5:10-6:21), Congress (6:21-7:11), Lexington to Bunker Hill (7:28-10:31), Ticonderoga guns (8:34, paid off 11:37), independence (12:28-13:19), and Brooklyn defeat (13:38-14:45).

## Strongest moves
1. **Dependency order.** The Seven Years' War is told first so that the tax demand (2:40: "we spent all our money protecting you") has a cause. The dependency chain is debt, tax, boycott, repeal, anger.
2. **Act out the rule.** The Stamp Act (3:16) is a shopkeeper scene with a stamp at every step, so the viewer sees why it is annoying instead of hearing about it.
3. **Visible repetition.** Tax, boycott, British business hurts, back down occurs twice (3:48-4:08, 4:53). The second time the narrator can skim, because the viewer predicts it.
4. **Plant and pay off.** The guns at Fort Ticonderoga (8:34-9:09) are called back with "remember all those guns?" at 11:37, then used on Dorchester Heights (12:12).
5. **Escalation ladder.** The Boston Massacre (5:26) goes from snowballs to rocks to oyster shells, making cause and effect gradual.

## Unusual
The hook is a rant and a misconception (Columbus), which both entertains and establishes the channel's tone. The Vikings aside answers a likely objection before it is made. Sponsor is separated by a "where was I" reset (1:35).

## Principle verdicts
1 confirmed (Columbus, school history). 5 confirmed. 7 and 9 confirmed through the tax loop. 11 confirmed (war debt first). 4 partly (shopkeeper is toy world, not stepped up). 8 partly: speech flat; scene length varies. 10 partly. 12 and 13 partly. 2, 3, 6: not observed.

## Velocity implications
- Scene builder: render a rule as a literal transaction with one visible step per rule clause.
- Orchestrator: keep a list of planted objects and call them back by name later; learner-model recall check.
- Ghost hands: run a cycle twice, then let the learner predict the third pass.
- Voice: anticipate the likely objection ("But OverSimplified...") in the learner's own voice.
- Caution: narrative history is low on notation, so the transfer is for memory structure only.
