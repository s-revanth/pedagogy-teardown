# World's Roundest Object (Veritasium, 11:50 of content)

**Arc.** Sensory hook (holding the sphere, 0:00), shock scale (Earth with 14 m relief, 1:02), a long story detour on the history of the kilogram (1:18-4:40), a failure (replicas drift by 50 micrograms, 4:56-6:04), the fix explained in an interview (atom counting, 6:52-9:15), a callback accuracy check (10:09), and a pun that closes the loop (11:14).

**Strongest moves**
1. *Detour as delayed answer (1:18-6:52).* The question "why spend a million euros" is answered by history, not physics. The learner needs the definition problem before the solution has meaning.
2. *Failure shown before solution (4:56-6:04).* Forty replicas, one weigh-in in 1948, another forty years later: a unit that moves. The 50 microgram figure is made concrete as a fingerprint.
3. *Scale the extreme (1:02).* Rescaling to Earth turns a precision spec into an image.
4. *Commit then honest-caveat (5:47).* The cause of drift is openly unknown, and the video says so.
5. *Callback to the original definition (10:09).* Testing the 1799 water definition against modern measurement (a rice grain off) closes the story as build-and-verify.

**What is unusual.** There is no notation at all and almost no concept ladder; the pedagogy is narrative causality (each step exists because the previous one failed). Interviews do the expert-depth work while the host plays the naive questioner ("You can't actually count how many are in there, can you?", 7:09). Humour (guillotine, a jab at non-metric countries) keeps attention through a history-heavy stretch.

**Principle verdicts.** 1 confirmed (object first). 2 confirmed (no symbols). 3 not observed. 4 partly (Earth analogy, no rigor step). 5 partly. 6 partly (counting dismissed, calculation used, 7:09). 7 contradicted: the viewer is told, not led to predict. 8 partly: idea rate is flat, no sign of slowing for hard parts. 9, 10 not observed. 11 confirmed (problem before solution). 12 confirmed (history, constants, pound). 13 partly.

**Velocity implications**
- Orchestrator: before a solution beat, require a failure or conflict beat; the solution should answer a pain the learner just felt.
- Scene builder: a "rescale to familiar" scene for any extreme magnitude.
