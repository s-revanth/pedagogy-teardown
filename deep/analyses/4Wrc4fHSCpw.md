# These are the asteroids to worry about (Veritasium, 20 min)

## Arc
Cold open on Chelyabinsk (0:08) and the embarrassing same-day miss of Duende (0:57). Three questions (2:06): why do we struggle to detect, how likely is a big hit, what can we do. Then origin of asteroids (2:25), detection and the opposition effect (4:26), prediction limits (6:08), Barringer crater (7:33), 10 km extinction event (10:09), risk arithmetic (11:51), size ladder (12:38), failed deflection ideas (14:39), evacuation (16:47), do surveys (17:50), sponsor (18:42).

## Strongest moves
1. **Roadmap by question (2:06).** Three questions answered in order; the third gets the longest section.
2. **Barringer as story (7:33-9:29).** A miner drills for 27 years after an iron meteorite that vaporized on impact. The story carries the physics: impacts are explosions, not stones in walls.
3. **Risk arithmetic with conditioning (11:51-12:21).** 1 in 100 million a year becomes 1 in a million per lifetime, then zero once we know no such rock is on course. Probability taught as a living number.
4. **Hopeless ladder (14:39-17:50).** Bomb, rocket, laser, foil, evacuation, each fail for stated reasons. The honest endpoint, "let's do the thing we can do first", feels earned.
5. **Opposition effect (4:58-5:47).** A statistic (85% found in 45 degrees opposite the sun) explains the Chelyabinsk miss.

## Unusual
Candid "no" from an expert on camera; most explainers end with a hopeful fix. Little visual teaching in the transcript; expert interview carries most of it. Sponsor is outro, tied back with a calendar gag.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 9, 10, 11, 12. Partly: 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 13. Not observed: 2, 3, 8. Speech-rate flatness gets no evidence for or against; the idea rate rises with stakes but never slows for new notation, since there is none.

## Velocity implications
- Allow the tutor to say "this is unsolved" plainly; model a failed-options ladder scene.
- Use story beats to carry physical intuition before any formula.
- Per-year to lifetime probability is a good compact numeracy micro-lesson.
- Open with the question list; eval that each question is answered in order.
- Keep concrete scale comparisons (school buses, Hiroshima multiples) in scene voiceover.
