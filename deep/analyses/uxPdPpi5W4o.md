# Why Are 96,000,000 Black Balls on This Reservoir? (Veritasium, 12 min)

## Arc
Paradox hook (0:00): shade balls are not mainly for evaporation. Narrator rides a boat across the pile, lists five questions (0:51), then answers them in order of increasing surprise. Core story: bromide plus chlorine plus sunlight makes bromate (2:59-4:23); only sunlight can be removed; failed covers; bird balls found (5:29); kiddie-pool test (6:17); why black (6:32); evaporation myth defused (8:57); cost; hexagon question; boba joke. No sponsor, no recap.

## Strongest moves
1. **Question backlog as roadmap (0:51).** Five viewer questions up front; each is later paid off. Evaporation is saved for 8:57, so the opening myth stays open for eight minutes.
2. **Rejection ladder (4:39-5:29).** Tarps, PVC trampoline, floating pipe, chipped pipe: each dies for a stated reason. By the time balls appear the solution looks forced. This is brute-force-first told by the practitioner.
3. **Three-ingredient reduction (4:23).** "We have bromide, chlorine, sunlight; only one can be removed." Viewer can solve it before being told.
4. **Cheap controlled test (6:17).** Three kiddie pools (sun, tarp, balls) make the claim checkable by a child.
5. **Myth planted then defused with a mechanism (8:57-10:02).** Black should heat water; answer is less surface and airflow, a cool underside, and an air gap like a double-paned window (9:29).

## Unusual
Almost no narration of theory; nearly all explanation comes from the three engineers on site, so exposition feels like conversation. Narrator plays the surrogate for the viewer's naive questions. The ending is a joke, not a takeaway.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1 (familiar objects), 5 (driving the boat), 6 (failed covers), 11 (problem, constraint, solution), 12 (bird balls, milk jugs). Partly: 4, 7, 10, 13. Not observed: 2, 3, 8, 9 (no notation, no repeated examples, no visible pace change).

## Velocity implications
- Add a rejection-ladder scene type with candidates failing with one-line reasons.
- Open lessons with a visible question list; tick off on screen as a roadmap/callback.
- Plant a misconception early, defer correction until the learner can object; eval for this.
- Let the learner physically push objects in the sim (drag through the balls) before explaining wobble.
- Prefer a tiny controlled comparison (three pools) as the proof scene.
