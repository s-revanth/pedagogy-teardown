# Which Way Is Down? (Vsauce, 26:10)

## Arc
Down pun (0:00), Sun-to-black-hole sandbox (0:38), mass as resistance (2:32), gravity and weight (3:40 to 6:41), apparent weight: ISS, balloon, buoyancy (7:17 to 9:32), down varies with bulge, spin, moon, lumps (9:48 to 12:48), why attract: feather, hammer, Apollo 15 (13:04 to 14:46), Einstein elevator (15:03), badge strings and ribbon test (16:03 to 20:35), space-time cone (21:14), slower time is down (24:00).

## Strongest moves
1. **Remove a variable (1:43 to 2:32).** Shrinks the Sun in a simulation, planets do not move, and the unchanged quantity (mass) becomes the clue.
2. **Ribbon test (17:46 to 19:33).** A strip of paper lies flat only on a straight path. Cone line works, base ring does not, sphere equator works, latitude does not. Geodesic is named after the viewer has seen four tries.
3. **Misconception stack (5:26, 7:39, 14:07).** Mass versus weight, weightless astronauts, heavy-falls-faster each get a counter-demo.
4. **Dimension reduction (21:31).** One space axis plus time on a sheet of paper, bent into a cone, makes falling a straight line.
5. **Callback (24:00 to 24:54).** The opening pun returns with slower time as the direction of down.

## Unusual
Longest of the five, and it gives an example of failure left in (feather and hammer in air, 13:47). It is built from objects in hand more than from graphics. Formula is spoken, not shown.
