# The Egg - A Short Story (kurzgesagt, 8:06)

**Arc.** A fictional dialogue in second person. You die (0:03), meet a plain, vague God figure (1:09), and learn reincarnation (2:03). Misconceptions fall in order: no Heaven or Hell, no blank slate (2:37). Then comes the water-glass analogy (3:18) and the reveal that you are every human (5:40). The purpose of the universe is stated at 5:06, and the final line names the egg (7:10).

**Strongest moves**
1. **Second-person role (0:03).** The viewer is the protagonist, so the stakes (family, dying) are personal before any idea appears.
2. **Character voices the objection (2:20-5:22).** Each question the viewer would ask arrives on cue: Hindus right? blank slate? meet myself? what about everyone else? Dialogue does the sequencing.
3. **Escalating reveal chain (5:40-6:36).** Lincoln and Booth, Hitler and victims, Jesus and followers. The same identity move repeats with rising moral weight until the implication, victimizing others is victimizing yourself, lands.
4. **Learner states the insight (5:40, 7:10).** The character says "I am everyone" and "it is an egg", and the narrator only confirms.
5. **Small analogy for a big claim (3:18).** A finger in water explains how a vast soul inhabits a small life.

**Unusual.** No visuals matter here; there is no notation or simulation. The caveat that some things are beyond your understanding (4:30) is used instead of filling gaps, and the explanation is a belief system, not a fact set.

**Principle verdicts.** 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 11 confirmed. 4 partly (one analogy). 8 partly (flat voice, reveal beats escalate). 3, 6, 12 not observed.

**Velocity implications**
- Cast the learner as a character in scenes for abstract or ethical topics.
- Have the tutor voice the learner's probable questions as dialogue, then answer.
- Use escalating repeated examples and have the learner finish the rule.
- Hold the metaphor's name until the learner offers it.
