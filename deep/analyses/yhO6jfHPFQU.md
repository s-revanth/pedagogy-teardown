# Simulating alternate voting systems (Primer, 14:04)

## Arc
Hook on two-party politics (0:00), roadmap with a promised table of flaw plus strategy per system (0:48), toy world of gray voter blobs in 1D then 2D issue space (1:37). Then plurality, IRV, approval, each tested on the same orange/green then purple scenario. Verdict and a vote-every-time sign-off (12:33).

## Strongest moves
1. **Fixed scene, swapped rule** (3:19, 6:33, 8:34). Orange vs green, then add purple, repeated under each system. The learner compares by watching the same stimulus change outcome. Plurality flips the winner, IRV matches strategic plurality, approval leaves totals unchanged. This is visible repetition across examples (principle 9).
2. **Find the counterexample yourself** (6:00, 9:35). He asks the viewer to pause and find the arrangement that breaks IRV, and the strategy as an approval voter. Then reveals center squeeze and the layers of strategy.
3. **Predict at the first surprise** (3:19). Add purple, what happens? Green wins, and only then the term spoiler effect appears (3:51). Concept before name.
4. **Strategy in layers** (9:53-11:29). Layer one is a rule (disapprove your least favorite); layer two is a story of mutual betrayal that produces the chicken dilemma, linked to hawk-dove from another video. Complexity is revealed one rule at a time.
5. **Commit and caveat together** (7:24-7:58, 12:33). Australia gives IRV a real-world check ("better, still two parties"); approval has no examples and he says so; yet plurality is called worst and he states his preference.

## Unusual
- The talk-through promises the structure up front (flaw + strategy per system) so each section fills in a known slot.
- Real-world evidence is cited at the end of each system, not saved for the end.
- Opinionated verdict, openly personal.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12. Partly: 4 (almost no analogy), 8 (speech steady; slowing comes from pauses and sims), 13. None contradicted.

## Velocity implications
- A reusable "same scenario, different rule" scene template with side-by-side outcomes.
- A find-the-flaw pause where ghost hands place candidates and the learner tries to break the rule first.
- Voice names the phenomenon only after the effect is seen.
- Track in the learner model whether they found the counterexample.
- Closing beat should commit to an answer with stated tradeoffs.
