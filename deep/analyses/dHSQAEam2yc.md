# WW1 - Oversimplified (Part 1) (dHSQAEam2yc, 6:24)

## Arc
A short, fast, comic narrative. It opens with an ironic statement of the era (0:02), lists grievances as gossip (0:18), tells the assassination as a near-miss with a reversal (0:50-1:07), runs the alliance chain as friends asking for backup (1:24-1:58), states Germany's plan in three steps (1:58), exposes its single flaw (2:19), then follows consequences: Belgium, Britain, the US, uniforms, eastern front, Marne, trenches. It stops in the middle of the story at the trench conditions list (5:55), with no recap or teaser captured.

## Strongest moves
1. **Plan then single flaw (1:58-2:19).** Germany's plan is stated as a clean sequence, then "just one problem" leads directly to the Belgium invasion and Britain joining. Causality comes from a character's intention failing.
2. **Spot the French soldier (3:16-3:35).** A tiny mini-game lets the viewer see the problem (bright blue and red uniforms) before the explanation of why columns were slaughtered. Principle 5 in miniature.
3. **Reversal turn (0:50-1:07).** Gives the plausible outcome ("the war never happens"), then negates it. It teaches that the event was contingent.
4. **Alliance chain as friendship (1:42-1:58).** The tangle of treaties is told as friends asking "you got my back?" and Britain answering "maybe". Analogy is never refined, which is a weakness.
5. **Trench cycle as ordered steps (5:06-5:55).** The only place the pace slows to rule-list: opposing trenches, shelling, rush, machine guns, repeat.

## Unusual
Very little structure for a teaching video: no roadmap, no questions, no repeated examples. Everything rides on voice, jokes, and a motivated chain of events. The one definition that matters (flanking, 4:48) is a one-sentence aside exactly when needed.

## Velocity implications
- Make "plan, flaw, consequence" a reusable beat template for causal topics.
- Add tiny prediction mini-games before the explanation (spot the soldier).
- Define a term in one sentence at point of need, as flanking is.
- An evaluation should flag explanations that stay at one idea per 15 seconds with no repetition, as this one does: engaging but with little retention scaffolding.
- Needs a recap beat; this video shows what an unfinished arc feels like.
