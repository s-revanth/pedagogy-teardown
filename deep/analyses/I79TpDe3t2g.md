# The Cold War Part 1 (OverSimplified): teardown

## Arc
Merch gag, Lenin and Stalin set-up, wartime allies, a mid-roll sponsor read at 2:59, then the postwar escalation: Eastern Europe, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin blockade, China, Korea, de-Stalinization, spying, space race, and finally the Wall and the Checkpoint Charlie standoff. No opening question; the structure is chronological with Berlin as the thread.

## Strongest moves
1. **Berlin as recurring pressure point (6:27, 13:25, 14:16).** The Cold War is framed at 6:11 as an economic battle, and Berlin is where it gets concrete. Each return adds stakes: East Berliners visit the West, then they defect, then the Wall.
2. **Replay and spot the flaw (13:57).** After Kennedy's firm line, the video rewinds, stretches the line out, and points at the slip. The viewer finds the cause of the Wall before the Wall is announced.
3. **Hollow-promise dialogue (4:02).** 'Free and fair' elections repeated in an ironic voice makes the satellite states legible without a definition of puppet government.
4. **Inching standoff (15:19).** De-escalation is acted as repeated 'back up one inch', a brute-force-first view of how crisis resolves.
5. **Repetition as pattern (10:08).** Three uprisings, each 'brutally suppressed', teach the pattern by sameness.

## What is unusual
Heavier on sketch humor than WW2 Part 1, with ad reads placed at jokes (the Wi-Fi bit leads into NordVPN). The 13:57 replay is rare: it treats the viewer as a detective. No notation, no quiz, no recap.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11. Partly: 1, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13. Not observed: 3. Speech rate is flat; slowdown appears in the final Berlin stretch through longer beats.

## Velocity implications
- Return to one concrete case with rising stakes.
- Build a replay scene where the tutor rewinds and the learner spots the slip.
- Step-by-step ghost-hand motion for gradual processes.
- Mark sarcasm so literal learners do not misread it.
- Add what is missing: an opening question and a recap.
