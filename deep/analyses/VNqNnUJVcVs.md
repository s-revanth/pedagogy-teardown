# Is Earth Actually Flat? (Vsauce, 10:31)

## Arc
Hook paradox (0:00) -> toy-world physics of a disc Earth (0:35-2:07) -> pivot "what if gravity isn't real" -> history myth (Columbus, 2:23) -> real evidence (bridge, Eratosthenes) -> Voliva and modern flat-earthers -> ad hoc vs best-fit theories (6:42) -> frame-dependent shape via cosmic-ray proton (7:01) -> crossword epistemology (8:11) -> Feynman close.

## Strongest moves
1. **Misconception-first with a twist (0:00-0:17).** The "right?" and "Wrong" aims at a bad argument for a true conclusion, which is rarer than attacking a false belief.
2. **Act-it-out toy world (0:35-1:48).** A simulated runner climbing an ever-steeper "hill" on a flat disc, and the punchline that stepping off the edge means rolling back. Simulation is the argument.
3. **Immediate honest caveat (1:48).** The model is declared physically impossible (it would collapse into a ball) before the viewer can over-believe it.
4. **Steelman (4:48).** Eratosthenes' shadows are reproduced by a near, small sun. The learner feels the evidence is underdetermined before being told so.
5. **Frame-shift (7:01-8:11).** A proton sees Earth as 17 m thick, so "flat to them, round to us". The crossword with two valid answers (cat or bat) makes the epistemology tangible.

## Unusual
No notation, no recap, no roadmap, no brute force. The video is an essay in stance, and its payoff is a changed attitude. Jokes (Big Globe, Poe's Law, the UN logo) carry idea-rate slowdown while speech rate stays flat.

## Velocity implications
- A "faithful wrong model" playbook: simulate the contrarian view, then name the flaw right away.
- Scene builder needs observer-frame toggles.
- Voice should flag model limits right after a fun demo.
- Evals should test underdetermination reasoning ("why do two models fit?"), not just the correct answer.
- Story detours (history myth, 1996 crossword) are cheap novelty-slowers worth modelling as optional beats the orchestrator inserts when the learner's attention drops.
