# The Closest Thing We Have to Alien Technology (Veritasium, 55 min)

## Arc
Hook by zoom (0:00) and an ant-sized laser thought experiment (1:04). Fab cycle in four steps (3:16), photolithography and diffraction demo (5:25), NA and Rayleigh (7:17), then a three-decade story of Kinoshita (9:14), Livermore (15:11), ASML (21:50): light budget, wavelength choice, tin droplet source, debris and hydrogen, pre-pulse, oxygen fix. High NA tour (41:30), logistics (49:32), essay on the unreasonable person (51:27), sponsor.

## Strongest moves
1. **Thought experiment before the machine (1:04).** Viewer is the ant with a laser; absurd but concrete, and everything later is a refinement of it.
2. **Demo before notation (5:25-8:22).** A real reticle and red/green laser pointers show diffraction. Numerical aperture is introduced at 7:17 as the sine of a visible angle; Rayleigh equation only named afterward.
3. **Obstacle-fix loop (19:56-40:41).** Each technical idea is the answer to a failed attempt (xenon 0.5% efficiency, tin debris, mirror drift, mirror cleaning). Light budget recomputed (4%, 8%, 0.00128%) so the viewer sees the same arithmetic reused.
4. **Scale analogies (2:12, 13:50, 33:42, 47:21).** Earth-size mirror with playing-card bump, Germany with 1 mm, dime on the Moon. The same device returns for high NA.
5. **Human story spine (12:28, 16:35, 36:11).** Laughed-off talks and repeated near-failures tie lessons together.

## Unusual
Long runtime with a roadmap-free arc, but the dissent story acts as one. Cross-domain reuse: nuclear blast formula fitting tin plasma (31:51). Presenter admits the engineers "knew" but only after repeated failure, no tidy answers; the aside that ASML did not sponsor the video (3:01) is a trust move.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13. Partly: 7, 8. Not observed: 3. On principle 8, speech stays brisk; slowdown comes through demos and analogies rather than slower talking.

## Velocity implications
- Reusable obstacle-fix scene with a running constraint meter (power, throughput).
- Gate formulas behind a demo; name symbols after the learner sees the quantity.
- Keep one scale-analogy device and reuse it across the lesson.
- Use named human stories for long sessions to bridge dense sections; surface them as breaks.
- Ghost hands should mark two-slit in-phase and out-of-phase spots as narration lands.
