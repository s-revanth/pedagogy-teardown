# You Actually Share Your Brain With a Silent Stranger (wfYbgdo8e-8)

## Structure arc
The video runs about five minutes. It goes hook (0:00-0:38), then the rule model (0:38), then a run of experiments (0:53-2:15), then the identity question (2:15-3:07). After that comes a fenced-off speculation section (3:07-3:58), a hedged conclusion (3:58), and a teaser for the collab with another channel (4:18). The surface is light and jokey, but the core is very compact: two rules, about six experiments, one question.

## Strongest moves
1. **A two-rule model, stated twice (0:00, 0:38).** In the opening Grey says each half controls the opposite side. At 0:38 he says "remember" and restates that rule, then adds the one asymmetry that matters: only the left half speaks. Every later result follows from these two rules, so the viewer never has to learn another symbol.
2. **Experiments acted out with voices (0:53-1:40).** Each experiment is staged as a scene. We hear "Nothing.", and a made-up reason for holding the object. That turns an abstract claim about the brain into a dialogue the viewer can watch happen.
3. **Escalation by kind, not by size (0:53 to 2:15).** The experiments test perception (naming a word), then action (picking up the object), then explanation (confabulation), then drawing two objects at once, then disagreeing answers. Each step raises the stakes until the "who is you" question at 2:15 lands.
4. **Turning it on the viewer (1:40, 3:40, 3:58).** First, healthy people confabulate too, and you have. Then, two of you are watching this video. Then, your objection right now is exactly what the left brain would do. This last one heads off disbelief by recasting it as evidence. It is a strong rhetorical move, though it is also unfalsifiable.
5. **Speculation fence (3:07 / 3:58).** "Speculation time" and "Speculation aside" bracket the conjecture, so the hedged conclusion stays separate from the established findings.

## What's unusual
There are no diagrams and no notation. The whole argument rests on a predictive model so small that the viewer can run the next experiment in their head. Most explainers add a new concept with each example. Here the model stays fixed and only the stimulus changes, which makes this almost a pure case of principle 9.

## Principle verdicts (brief)
- Confirmed: 1 (familiar start), 2 (minimal symbols), 5 (acting out), 9 (same model across examples), 10 (examples grow), 11 (dependency order), 13 (simple enough for a kid).
- Partly: 4, 7, 8, 12. On 8, the idea rate slows in the speculation section, but the delivery does not slow.
- Not observed: 3, 6.

## Velocity implications
- Build a split-stage scene primitive with a per-pane visibility mask. Each experiment is then just a stimulus routed to one pane.
- Restate the governing rule right before any beat that depends on it.
- Fence speculation in the voice script ("speculation starts", "speculation ends"). The learner model can then store these claims as conjectural.
- Check understanding by prediction: after two experiments, have the learner predict the third outcome before it is shown.
