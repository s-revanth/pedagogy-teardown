# Kurzgesagt: The Bacteriophage (YI3tsmFsrOg)

## Arc
War hook (0:00), what a phage is (0:15), why they are harmless to us (0:47), host specificity and cruise-missile analogy (1:22), infection walk-through (1:37-2:32), antibiotics story and superbugs (2:32-4:01), two objections answered (4:17, 4:54), catch-22 and patient case (5:30), caveat and callback (6:13), credits.

## Strongest moves
1. **A single metaphor spine (0:00, 2:48, 4:34).** War, monsters and weapons frame everything. Bacteria were our phages, antibiotics were our weapon, and phages are guided missiles. New facts attach to a story already in the viewer's head.
2. **Voiced objections (4:17, 4:54).** "Hold on, how could injecting viruses be a good idea?" appears right where a viewer would object. Both objections are answered with a reason (immunity through difference, bacteria's costly trade-off).
3. **Step-by-step infection (1:37-2:14).** Attach, puncture, inject, takeover, manufacture, burst. A sequence made concrete with vivid verbs.
4. **Analogy contrast (4:34).** Carpet bombing versus guided missiles makes the selectivity argument in one sentence.
5. **Honest caveat then callback (6:13).** Treatment is experimental and funding is lacking, then the hook "deadliest being" returns as the hope.

## Unusual
Terminology is minimal ("endolysin" is the only new word and gets one gloss). The first question arrives late (4:17), after four minutes of narrative. The tone is humorous-dark, using "genocide for breakfast" to stay memorable.
