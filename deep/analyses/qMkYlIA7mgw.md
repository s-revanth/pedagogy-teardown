# CGP Grey: The US-Canada Border (qMkYlIA7mgw)

## Arc
Hook paradox (0:00): the longest, straightest, most boring border is secretly bizarre. Define the no-touching-zone (0:16). Break an assumption (0:33): it is not straight, it is about 900 zig-zag segments. Role-play the surveyor (0:52). Disputed islands as a comic aside (1:43). Zoom on one oddity, the lake tick (2:17), with a causal story: 1783 negotiators bordered blind because the key area hid under a map inset (2:33). Generalize to Vancouver Island and Point Roberts (3:26-4:01), mirror case on the east coast, then a satellite-photo tip. The transcript is cut at 4:19.

## Strongest moves
1. Misconception then break (0:33): "deforested, but straight? Not at all." Cheap, immediate, and it drives curiosity.
2. Second-person role (0:52): you hold a compass and string. The learner feels why straight lines fail before any explanation.
3. One cause, many cases (2:33-4:01): bad map, then vague treaty, then correction creates a jog. The same story logic then explains Vancouver Island and Point Roberts.
4. Zoom from whole to one tick (2:17): show-full-complexity-then-zoom.
5. Human-cost ending of the ladder (3:43): kids cross the border four times a day.

## Unusual
No notation, no quiz, no brute force. It teaches entirely through story and dry humor. Speech is fast and flat; the slowdown comes from longer story beats (the 1783 map, about 50 s) rather than slower talking.
