# OverSimplified: The Napoleonic Wars, Part 1 (29 min)

## Arc
Sponsor plug over a birth-scene cold open (0:00), then a question-shaped hook: great conquerors have grand origins, so how did a nobody from Corsica get here (0:36-1:18). Childhood and school are acted out (1:48-3:28), a blocked promotion makes the old social order concrete (3:28), and the Revolution arrives as the answer to Napoleon's problem (4:02). The rest is a chain of campaigns: Italy, Egypt, coup, reforms, empire, then the Second to Fourth Coalition wars. A mid-roll sponsor (15:18) splits it; the narrator resumes with a one-line recap (16:23).

## Strongest moves
1. **Act it out, literally.** The promotion form (3:28), the Austrian ambush and the fake "baby boy" letter (23:23) replace exposition with dialogue. Abstract structures (nepotism, a trap) become a scene.
2. **Characters for abstractions.** France and Austria chat like friends (5:58); Prussia gets a voice (26:56). Alliances become memorable without any diagram.
3. **Named-factor countdown.** At 13:10 the narrator says one trait of Napoleon's greatness is loyalty, and promises more ("one of them"). At 22:02 he pays off "one of the biggest reasons, speed", then explains corps, then stages a maneuver (22:50, "Would you like to see him do it?"). This is a roadmap, build-and-verify and show-then-do.
4. **Visible repetition.** Austria loses to Napoleon three times (First, Second, Third Coalition). The pattern is felt before it is named.
5. **Callback to hook.** The "what comes after Emperor" ambition joke (20:06) returns to the opening framing of conquerors.

## Unusual
No notation or symbols exist; all load is carried by voice acting and humor. Misconception handling is a joke format (Alps painting at 13:42, height at 9:33). The honest-caveat appears for unsavory facts, such as scaled-back women's rights (17:25).

## Principle verdicts
1 confirmed (childhood first). 5 confirmed. 7 and 9 confirmed through repeated defeats. 4 partly: nations-as-people never steps toward rigor. 8: speech stays brisk; novelty slows via longer scenes (Austerlitz 23:23-25:02) rather than speed. 10 and 11 only partly, since order is chronological. 12 partly (the painting, Caesar). 2, 3, 6: not observed.

## Velocity implications
- Scene builder: persistent avatar and voice per entity so dialogue replaces exposition.
- Orchestrator: plant a promised list and pay off one item per example, giving built-in structure.
- Evals: confirm a repeated pattern shows at least three times before the tutor names it.
- Voice: keep wpm flat; vary beat length and example count.
- Caution: this is narrative history with no abstraction ladder, so transfer to math or code is limited to hooks, repetition and callbacks.
