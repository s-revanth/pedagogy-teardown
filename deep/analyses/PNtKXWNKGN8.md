# Simulating Supply and Demand (Primer)

## Arc
Roadmap (0:00), blue seller and orange buyer blobs with value bars (0:15), a $40/$20/$30 worked trade defining surplus (1:21), then four scenes: 1+1, 2 buyers/1 seller (4:08), 2 buyers/3 sellers (5:17), 3+3 (6:11). Equilibrium and curves at 7:08, efficiency at 9:00, regulation arguments at 10:03, outro.

## Strongest moves
1. **One actor at a time (2:12-6:11).** Each added blob comes with "what do you expect to happen?" The viewer finds that buyer competition favors sellers (4:44), then the mirror case (5:55).
2. **Concrete before term (1:21-1:56).** Surplus is introduced after specific dollars. A pause (1:56) stresses that value became quantifiable by comparing alternatives.
3. **Daily rule list as interface (2:44-3:48).** Random order, adjust expectations after each day, accept slightly worse at day end. The learner gets simulation rules before seeing results.
4. **Failure shown (6:36).** With 3 and 3 only two rockets sell. The lowest buyer and highest seller cannot match, so the setup that looked symmetrical is not.
5. **Curves as compression (7:39).** Supply and demand curves arrive only when blobs are too many to display.

## Unusual
The last third is a map of arguments, not a conclusion (8:27). It names conditions for ideal markets (10:19) and an explicit limit of surplus as measure (10:52). Lecture-light and politically scoped; ends with a humorous callback (11:57).

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11. Partly: 3, 4, 8, 12, 13. No timing data for 8; idea rate rises at 7:08 and 8:43 after sim scenes.

## Velocity implications
- One-actor-at-a-time scene builder with a live surplus tally.
- Voice names a term only after the scene displays its value.
- Orchestrator asks a prediction before each added actor.
- Evals check that the tutor states what the model leaves out.
