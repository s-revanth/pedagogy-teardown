# You Can Never Become Pope (CGP Grey, 5:09)

## Structure arc
Hook paradox (thin official requirements, 0:00) -> reframe via unofficial rule (0:17-0:33) -> three-rung ladder (priest 0:33, bishop 1:07, cardinal 2:47) -> conclave mechanics (3:18-4:27) -> numeric funnel recap (4:27). It is a pure procedural explainer: no notation, no formulas, one running path.

## Strongest moves
1. **Second-person protagonist (0:00 onward).** The viewer is the candidate. Every rule becomes an obstacle "you" must clear, which turns a list of canon-law facts into a quest with stakes.
2. **Unofficial-rule reveal (0:17-0:33).** Grey contrasts formal eligibility (any Catholic man) with practice (no non-cardinal in 600 years). This reframes the problem and justifies the starting rung. It doubles as a misconception fix: you cannot run for pope like a president.
3. **Repeated stage template.** Each rung gives requirements, a scarcity number, and who selects. By the bishop stage the viewer knows what slots to expect, so new content lands in a familiar frame.
4. **Run-on gate recap (2:31).** One breathless sentence lists every condition (retirement timing, secret list, Nuncio, congress, pope veto) and ends in "poof". Compounded improbability is felt through rhythm, not stated as math.
5. **Numeric funnel close (4:27).** Half the population, 1 billion, 400,000, 5,000, 200, then 2/3 of cardinals. The title claim is proven by the shrinking counts.

## What is unusual
There is no analogy-to-rigor step and no concept to derive. All the teaching is in sequencing and pacing a bureaucratic pipeline. Humor (carrier pigeons 3:18, HD cameras watching a chimney 3:54) works as a memory anchor for procedural detail. Misconception fixes come in small asides (cardinals are bishops, 2:47) rather than as framing.

## Principle verdicts (brief)
Confirmed: 1 (president, corporate ladder), 5 (literal role-play as candidate), 10 (shrinking rungs), 11 (strict dependency order), 13 (plain language). Partly: 3, 4, 7, 9, 12. Not observed: 2, 6, 8. Caveat: the video rarely explains *why* rules exist (why celibacy, why 2/3), so the depth half of 13 is weak.
