# How To Catch A Cheater With Math (Primer, 23 min)

## Arc
Blob coin game and a rumor of trick coins (0:00). Viewer judges five-flip samples (0:31). Three design goals are set (1:38), then built by brute force one flip at a time (2:33), a 5% threshold (4:14), a 1000-blob simulation (5:03), vocabulary (6:06), power and effect size (8:18), distributions (10:47), a computer search that finds 23 flips and 16 heads (14:02), two worked blobs and the p-value (14:53), trade-offs (17:28), a staged failure (18:54), and a framework summary (21:20).

## Strongest moves
1. **Design goals before method (1:38).** The viewer is asked to invent a test to meet three goals, with a pause. The method (hypothesis testing) is named only as the thing being built.
2. **Flip by flip (2:33 to 4:46).** Heads, heads, heads, with the innocent-blob chance halving each time (one in four, one in eight, one in 32). The threshold emerges from repetition instead of a definition.
3. **Label every choice (4:29, 8:50, 9:21, 9:53).** The 5%, 80%, and 75% are each called arbitrary, and the narrator says to track what is a choice and what is an assumption.
4. **Vocabulary with a warning (6:06 to 8:02).** Jargon arrives after the concept, and for false positive rate the narrator admits his own brain misreads the denominator and keeps plain wording.
5. **Staged assumption trap (18:54 to 21:04).** A mystery group is 90% cheaters using 60% coins, so the 75% assumption fails and only a quarter are caught. The narrator then asks the viewer if they remembered the assumption, and says the trick was deliberate. A line at 19:09 calls out prediction fatigue.

## Unusual
The mistake is built into the video as the lesson. The p-value is introduced as "kind of" a false positive rate and only after two blobs are tested. The binomial formula is deliberately deferred (11:54). Trade-offs among the three goals (17:28 to 18:39) show that fixing any two limits the third.

## Velocity implications
- Let the learner design before the standard answer is shown.
- Record every number as a choice or an assumption and quiz recall of assumptions before predictions.
- Scene: paired bar graphs with a draggable threshold and live category counts.
- Voice: warn on confusing terms; delay jargon.
- Evals: require a planned failure case that exposes a hidden assumption, followed by an explicit debrief.
