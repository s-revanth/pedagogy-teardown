# Pentagons Are Terrible at One Thing (thOifuHs6eY)

## Structure arc
The video opens with a joke claim: hexagons are the "bestagons" (0:00). It then grounds the claim in the bee's economics, where wax costs 8 units of honey (0:15). Tiling is defined (0:46), the regular polygons are enumerated, and the honeycomb result follows (2:22). From there it tours domains: bee eyes and the human retina (2:53), Saturn (3:26), snowflakes and ice (4:31), and graphene (5:22). At 5:53 it introduces a second mechanism, 120-degree joints, and applies it to basalt, bubbles, pencils and honeycomb panels (6:09-7:17). It finishes with game boards (7:34) and a mock-cult call to spread the order (8:22).

## Strongest moves
1. **Enumerate before declaring the winner (0:49-1:04).** Complicated tiles use more wall, so drop them. Only three regular shapes tile without gaps, and pentagons leave gaps. This is a brute-force elimination, kept tidy and fast.
2. **The counting reveal (1:35, 2:37).** Grey counts "one, two, three, four, five, six" over triangles, irregular tilings and circle packings until a hexagon appears. By the second time, viewers count along. This is visible repetition that turns into discovery.
3. **A second mechanism, added late (5:53-6:27).** Once the min-wall idea has been used up, he adds joint stability. Then he says to look again at the tiling: it is nothing but stable joints. The same picture gets a new reading, which shows multiple decompositions in action.
4. **Raising the objection the example invites (6:27).** If graphene is so strong, how does a pencil write? The answer is that it is strong in-plane and weak between layers. That answer leads straight into honeycomb panels.
5. **The catchphrase as the video's spine.** "Bestagon" comes back after each domain, so the refrain itself is the structure people remember.

## What's unusual
The rigor is deliberately thin. The honeycomb conjecture is asserted, not shown, and Saturn's hexagon is left as an open mystery with an alien joke. The order of examples follows variety, not difficulty: planet, then atoms, then aircraft, then board games. That contradicts principle 10. What the video lacks in depth it replaces with persuasion through ubiquity. It also has almost no notation. The only formal element is root two at 7:50, used in a disarmingly concrete way: a diagonal move on a square board covers 1.41 spaces.

## Velocity implications
- Build a scene primitive that highlights hidden structure: a counter steps through neighbors and the voice counts along.
- Guide two reveals, then hand the third to the learner, with ghost hands only if they time out.
- Add a "one mechanism, many domains" playbook. Tie every domain back to a named mechanism, not just to a slogan.
- Evals should check that the learner can state the mechanism, because a catchphrase is easy to repeat without the understanding behind it.
