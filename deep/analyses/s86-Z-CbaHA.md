# The Banach-Tarski Paradox (Vsauce, 24 min)

## Arc
Fake chocolate paradox (0:00), debunk (0:33), real theorem (1:06), infinity primer (2:16-8:04: countable vs uncountable, Cantor diagonal, evens, Hilbert's hotel), hotel on a circle (8:04), Hyperwebster (9:16), the sphere construction (11:17-18:44), caveat about physical reality (19:01), philosophical close, book plugs.

## Strongest moves
1. **Debunk the fake version (0:33-1:22).** The video first shows a well-known fake paradox (the chocolate bar), explains the cheat, and then says the real theorem has no cheat. This builds suspicion that Banach-Tarski must also be a trick, and the later proof is read against that suspicion.
2. **One trick across four settings (6:41-11:17).** Hilbert's hotel shifts guests to absorb a hole; the circle shifts irrational-step points; the Hyperwebster drops a leading letter; the sphere then does the same with rotations. By the final proof the shift trick is familiar, which is the engine of the whole video (principle 9, 11).
3. **Brute force before the diagonal (3:40-4:30).** It tries to count reals from zero and stalls at 'what comes next?' before presenting Cantor's diagonal. The learner feels the failure that motivates the technique.
4. **Notation late and tiny (11:51-13:56).** Only four rotations (L, R, U, D), a no-backtracking rule list, and a note that words are written right to left. Four examples are worked and colour-coded (13:03), which is visible repetition before the general case.
5. **Honest caveat (19:56).** It admits pieces would need infinite complexity, so it cannot happen physically, and the physics link is only suggested by some papers. The philosophical close is a commit-to-opinion beat.

## Unusual
Very long (about 13 minutes of preparation) before the proof, but the speech rate does not change. The slowing comes from simplified idea steps, the repetition of the trick, and the delayed notation. The proof is stated with geometric animation rather than algebra and glosses details (pole fixes, centre point) with 'no worries'.

## Principle verdicts
1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 confirmed. 5, 7, 12, 13 partly. 3 not observed.

## Velocity implications
- Orchestrator: plan the sequence of stepping stones around one reusable trick.
- Scene builder: colour-coded pieces; keep notation to the minimum symbols and introduce them only after the concept.
- Learner model: when the brute-force attempt stalls, trigger the neat method.
- Voice: include an honest caveat beat near the end.
- Evals: for fake-then-real hooks, check the debunk is explicit and the stepping stones precede the proof.
- Caveat: the proof glosses technical details; Velocity should flag skipped steps rather than copy this.
