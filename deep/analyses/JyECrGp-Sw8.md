# What If We Detonated All Nuclear Bombs at Once? (Kurzgesagt, 7:06)

## Arc
Mock-serious hook (0:01). Quantify the arsenal (15,000 warheads, 0:20). Convert to cities (about 3 bombs per city, 4,500 cities, half of humanity, 1,500 bombs left: "overkill", 0:57-1:14). Build the pile (a warehouse in the Amazon, 1:32), anchor energy to Manhattan in TNT and 15x Krakatoa (1:51-2:27). Countdown and effects (2:27-3:49): Amazon lost, humans survive. Escalate with all the Earth's uranium, 10 billion Hiroshimas (4:07), same countdown and effects at larger scale: extinction (5:48). Dark coda: planet heals, future life confused by a thin radioactive layer (6:41).

## Strongest moves
1. **Act it out literally (2:27, 4:40).** "Three, two, one" and a second-person tour of the blast, fire, shock wave and aftermath.
2. **Same template at two magnitudes (2:27-3:49 vs 4:40-5:48).** Fireball, blast, fire, fallout, climate repeat, so the threshold between regional disaster and extinction shows by comparison.
3. **Familiar anchors for huge numbers (1:32-2:27).** Manhattan rebuilt from TNT, Krakatoa, the dinosaur asteroid.
4. **Crude estimate first (0:57).** Three bombs per city gives a quick, assumption-flagged answer before the spectacle.
5. **Humor as pacing (0:01, 1:14, 6:59).** "Gathered scientists to calculate"; "overkill"; confused future geologists.

## Unusual
No notation, almost no teaching of concepts; the lesson is magnitude intuition. The answer is stated upfront in effect: first pile, humans survive; second, they do not. Ending is cosmic indifference instead of a moral.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12. Partly: 4, 7, 8 (flat speech in transcript), 13. Not observed: 3.

## Velocity implications
- "What-if slider" scene rerunning one effect template at several magnitudes.
- Every large number gets a familiar anchor within one beat (eval).
- Orchestrator playbook "counterfactual escalation": baseline, anchor, escalate, aftermath.
- Ask the learner to predict the second run from the first.
