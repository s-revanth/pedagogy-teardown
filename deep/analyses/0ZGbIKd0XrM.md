# Simulating Natural Selection (Primer, 10 min)

## Arc
The video opens by saying earlier blobs had assigned birth and death chances, which is artificial, so it builds an environment (0:33) and runs it without mutation (1:05). It then turns on one trait, speed (1:54), predicts, runs, and states the first lesson (3:11). Size and sense are added (4:25), all three run together with a 3D trait graph (5:53), then food is cut (7:08) and ramped down (8:00). It ends with "the environment matters" and a recap (8:42 to 9:22).

## Strongest moves
1. **Baseline before variation (1:05).** The world runs with no mutation first, showing growth to carrying capacity. Later changes are read against that.
2. **Cost with every benefit (2:14, 4:25, 5:36).** Speed wins races but costs energy; size eats others but costs cubed energy; sense avoids danger but costs per step. No trait is obviously best, so the prediction prompt at 2:14 (it lists sprinting, patience, balance, or both) is real.
3. **Anti-teleology asides (3:27, 3:44).** Mid-sim it corrects "individuals evolve" (Pokemon joke) and notes the population shrank as the average got faster, so selection is not for the good of the species.
4. **Presenter surprised, three times (6:34, 7:08, 8:24).** Speed shifted without its rule changing, sense was not selected as expected, and sparse food made speed valuable. Each time the narrator says he expected otherwise, which models honest reasoning and sells the closing line that selection ignores opinions.
5. **Ramp the change (7:44 to 8:00).** An abrupt cut to ten food collapses the population, so it rewinds and reduces food gradually. This protects the lesson from being a shock artifact.

## Unusual
Almost no notation; the only formula (energy = size cubed times speed squared, 4:43) is justified by volume scaling and a kinetic-energy comparison. No verbal analogy and no quiz that gets answered by the viewer. The conclusion is deliberately "I can't predict it".

## Velocity implications
- Label every parameter with benefit and cost.
- Run a no-variation baseline first.
- Voice should state an expectation, then say when the run contradicts it.
- Offer a gradual ramp when a parameter change collapses a demo.
- Evals: check that a misreading (individuals evolve) is addressed at the moment the sim shows it.
