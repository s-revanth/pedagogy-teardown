# The Largest Star in the Universe (Kurzgesagt, 11:47)

## Arc
A catalogue with a spine. It opens on the question (0:01), starts at Earth for scale, then climbs a mass ladder: gas giants, brown dwarfs, red dwarfs, Sun-like stars, blue stars. At 4:31 it asks "is this the formula, more mass means bigger?" and breaks it with R136a1 (315 solar masses, only about 30 solar radii). The pivot is that the biggest stars come from dying stars (giants, hypergiants), not from heavier ones. It ends with an honest "we don't know the winner," a supernova and rebirth loop, a silent replay, and an app ad.

## Strongest moves
1. **Break the obvious rule (4:31-5:53).** After four examples where size rises with mass, the viewer has a pattern. The R136a1 counterexample then forces a second variable (life stage). Mass limit explained at 5:04 (radiation blows off extra gas).
2. **Fixed template per object (3:10-4:15, 6:44-7:35).** Each star gets mass, radius, brightness, lifespan. Repetition makes comparison automatic and the outlier visible.
3. **Honest caveat up front (2:05).** "Comparing babies to adults" admits the comparison is unfair before it matters.
4. **Unknown as answer (8:11-8:44).** No crowned winner; measurement error is taught as content.
5. **Brute force joke (0:35).** Throwing 90 Jupiters together still does not give a star, which teaches threshold by failed attempt.
6. **Scale made bodily (9:18).** 8.7 hours around it at light speed, 500 years by plane, fills Saturn's orbit. Then a wordless replay (10:10).

## Unusual
No notation at all; ratios stay in words. The surprise is the structural pivot, not a visual trick. The ending resists a tidy conclusion.

## Principle verdicts
Confirmed: 1, 2, 6, 9, 10, 11, 13. Partly: 4, 5, 7 (resolution told, not discovered), 8 (speech flat in the transcript; idea rate slows at the pivot), 12. Not observed: 3.

## Velocity implications
- Playbook "ladder-then-break": run a rule across 4+ examples with a constant card, then inject a counterexample scene.
- Pause and slow idea rate at the counterexample, not on list items.
- Have the learner predict the next object before reveal.
- Eval: does the lesson state fairness caveats about its own comparisons.
