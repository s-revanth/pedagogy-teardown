# Kurzgesagt: The Black Hole Bomb and Black Hole Civilizations

## Arc
A short (8 minute) goal-driven build. Hook and cheat code (0:00), why spin changes the story (0:33-1:59), the ringularity and ergosphere (1:59-3:15), the energy steal via the rocket trade (3:15-5:03), the black hole bomb (5:26-7:21), and a far-future coda (7:21-8:01). Note: the transcript has a gap between 0:33 and 1:03.

## Strongest moves
1. **Cheat-code goal frame (0:00-0:33, 3:15).** The viewer is given a goal (get energy out of the thing that keeps energy), so each fact serves it. It also opens with the misconception that all energy is trapped, confirms it, then says spin changes everything.
2. **Analogy ladder (2:56, 3:53, 4:30).** The same fluid theme changes with the physics: sliding down a hole for the static case, a deadly drain for the ergosphere, a whirlpool you can swim with, then a wave pool for the rocket trade. Each is one notch closer. The viewer never loses the picture, and the final analogy is the one that explains the actual gain.
3. **Derive the odd term (1:59).** A point cannot rotate, so the singularity must be a ring, hence "ringularity". A tiny contradiction makes the strange name feel inevitable and memorable.
4. **Literal build (5:26-6:42).** Two parts, a fast-spinning hole and a mirror, scaled with a number (10 cm asteroid-metal shell) and tied to a Dyson sphere. Then "superradiant scattering" is defined only after the ball-and-wall image (6:05), and so reads as a label.
5. **Fork at the end (6:42).** Open windows for endless energy, or leave closed and detonate. One mechanism, two outcomes, which makes it stick.

## What's unusual
Terms are nearly always deferred behind a physical stand-in, with a joke gloss ("fancy science words meaning..."). No equations at all, yet the exponential-growth claim is stated plainly. The ending ties back to the hook with a hopeful line instead of a recap.
