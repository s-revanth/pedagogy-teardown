## Narration

<<<NARRATION
What color is a mirror? It sounds like a silly question, because a mirror is supposed to show you the world, not have a color of its own. But take a second. Most mirrors look silvery, a little cool, sometimes faintly blue. Is that the mirror, or is it something else? Let's find out.

Short answer first. A mirror doesn't have a color the way a painted wall does. It has no pigment in it. It is a surface that sends light back, and whatever color you see comes from the light that hits it.

To see why, start with what reflection is. Light travels in straight lines until it hits something. Some surfaces absorb it, turning that energy into heat, and others bounce it back. A painted wall absorbs most colors and reflects only a few. That's why it looks red, or blue, or green. A mirror is designed to reflect almost everything it receives, so it sends the light back without keeping much of it for itself.

Try this in your head, and then picture the real version. Shine a plain white flashlight at a mirror. The beam comes back white, or close to it, and the room looks the way it normally does, just doubled in your eye. Now shine a red light at the same mirror. The reflection is red. Shine a blue light, and it's blue. The mirror doesn't add a color. It just returns whatever you give it.

That's why the answer depends on the light. Put a mirror under a warm yellow lamp, and it looks warm. Put it under a cool blue light, and it looks cool. Your eye is judging the mirror by the light bouncing off it, and the light in a room is always changing.

You can check this at home. Put a sheet of red cellophane over a flashlight, aim it at a mirror in a dim room, and the spot on the wall looks red. The mirror looks red wherever the beam lands. Pull the cellophane off, and the reflection goes back to white. Nothing about the mirror changed. Only the light did.

So why does a typical mirror look silvery? Here's the trick. The glass itself is clear. The reflective layer is a thin coating of metal, often aluminum or silver, applied to the back of the glass so it covers the surface evenly. Metal reflects most visible wavelengths in roughly equal amounts, so it sends back a broad mix that your eye reads as a neutral, bright silver. There's no dominant color to speak of, so the mirror looks colorless.

Mirrors don't always look like that, though. Some are made with coatings that reflect certain wavelengths more strongly. A gold-toned mirror, for example, has a coating that sends back more of the yellows and reds, so it looks warm and golden. Some decorative mirrors use tinted glass that absorbs part of the light on its way in and back out. Those mirrors really do look colored, but the color comes from the coating or the glass, not from some hidden paint layer. Think of tinted sunglasses for comparison. They don't add a color. They subtract one, by absorbing part of the light. A tinted mirror does the same thing, except the light passes through the glass twice, once on the way in and once on the way back out.

Real mirrors are never perfect either. A typical one reflects most of the visible light and absorbs a small amount. Over time the coating can tarnish or wear, and the reflection drifts toward a slight tint. Thick glass can look a little green at its edges, because ordinary glass contains a bit of iron that soaks up some red light. Museums that restore old mirrors run into this directly. When the silver backing flakes away, the glass shows dark patches, and the reflection looks grey and dull, so restorers have to rebuild the coating carefully. So if you're asking what color a particular mirror is, the honest answer depends on its coating, its glass, and the light in the room.

Now back to the start. A mirror has no paint, no pigment, no color of its own in the way a wall does. It's a surface that sends light back. Give it red light, you get red. Give it white light, you get white. The silver look is just the result of a metal layer that reflects nearly everything evenly.

So what color is a mirror? It's the color of whatever light is hitting it. And the next time someone asks you that question, you can ask them what light is shining on it.
NARRATION>>>
