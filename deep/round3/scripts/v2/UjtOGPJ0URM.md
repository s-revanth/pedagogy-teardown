## Narration
<<<NARRATION
Finding alien life would be bad news, and the reason has to do with our own future. The one exception would be life that shares our origin, carried between planets inside rocks, because then you would learn very little about the road ahead. Any other kind of alien life would tell us something unsettling about where we are heading, and that is the story you are about to hear.

Let's begin with a puzzle that has bothered astronomers for decades. The Milky Way holds hundreds of billions of stars, and many of those stars have planets, so there are a huge number of possible homes for life. The universe itself is about 13.8 billion years old, and Earth is about 4.5 billion years old, which gives every one of those homes plenty of time. If life starts easily, then somewhere out there it should have started long ago, and some of it should have grown clever enough to cross the gaps between stars. So where is everybody? That silence is the paradox we need to explain.

One possible answer is the great filter. A filter is a step that almost nothing gets past, and the name comes from the idea of a sieve that catches most of what pours through it. Somewhere on the road from dead chemicals to a galaxy full of travellers, there may be at least one gate so hard that most worlds never pass through it. The whole argument is about where that gate sits, and whether it is already behind us.

Picture a long corridor with gates along it, one gate for every step that life had to take. Depending on how finely you slice the steps, there might be four gates or four hundred. If the hard gate is behind you, you are one of the lucky few who got through. If the hard gate is ahead of you, the corridor is a trap, and reaching this far guarantees nothing about the rest of the walk. The position of that one gate changes everything about what the silence means. Two people can hear the same silence and draw opposite conclusions, depending on where you place the gate in your mind.

Let's test the gates in order, starting at the beginning. The first gate is the jump from chemistry to life. Earth cooled enough for life around four billion years ago, and the earliest traces of life are nearly as old as the stable surface itself. If that step came quickly here, it may be an easy gate. But there is only one example, so no one can be sure whether it was luck or routine.

The second gate is complex cells. Simple cells, the kind without a nucleus, ruled the planet for roughly two billion years before anything more complicated appeared. Then complex cells showed up, and they became the ancestors of every plant, animal and fungus alive today. That long wait looks like a gate that opened only once, and only after a very long delay.

The third gate is many-celled life. The first complex animals and plants appear only about 600 million years ago, a far shorter wait than the one before it. Some researchers think that step was easy once complex cells existed. Others think it needed a fortunate sequence of events. Either way, it took most of Earth's history to reach that point, and much of that history belonged to simple cells.

The fourth gate is a species that builds radio and rockets. We have been able to do that for about a century. Set against four billion years, that is a blink of an eye. Radio was first used in the early 1900s, so the whole gate is younger than most of your family history. Either this is the hardest gate of all, or it is the one that lies right after us. Keep these four rungs in mind, because they come back later in the story, and they matter more than you might expect.

Now it is your turn. Pause here and make a guess. Is the great filter behind us, meaning the hard steps are already done? Or is it ahead of us, meaning the hardest step is still to come? Pick one before going on, because the next part depends on your answer.

Now the logic. If the filter is behind us, the universe can be full of simple life and still hold no travellers, and our survival would be a rare win. If the filter is ahead of us, then finding simple life elsewhere is bad news, because it shows that the early steps are common. Microbes on Mars, if they turned out to be a second and unrelated start to life, would mean the early gates are open, and the hard gate must be waiting ahead of us.

That feels like doom for a simple reason. If life is easy, the easy steps have happened many times over, and the sky still looks empty. The explanation most often offered is that most civilizations get stopped before they can spread. Something ahead of us has already stopped them, or will stop us in the same way, and that is the doom the title refers to.

Every candidate explanation is a guess, and each one should be labelled as such. Life could be so rare that we are simply early. Complex cells could have been a fluke that we can never repeat in a laboratory. Technology could be the trap, and the civilizations that learn to build radio also learn to build the tools that end them. Most of these cannot be checked from here, so the honest label for each one is an educated guess with a reason behind it.

The last candidate is the most unsettling. A civilization that learns to split atoms, or to engineer living things, gains the power to wreck its own planet. If that is the common pattern, then the gate lies in the step just after radio, and the long silence makes a grim kind of sense. That is a bleak thought, but it makes a prediction, and predictions can be checked against evidence.

Let's go back to the first three rungs, because they can settle the argument. Suppose someone finds that chemistry on Mars started independently of ours. Then the first rung is no longer a gate, and the hard step has moved forward. Suppose instead that the rover finds only dead rock. Then the first gate looks hard, and the filter might be behind us after all. Mars is only about 55 million kilometres away at its closest, so the test is within reach. The first three rungs are the test, and the test is coming.

Notice what the test cannot prove. A dead Mars would not prove that the gate is behind us, and a living Mars would not prove that the gate is ahead. These discoveries change the odds, and they are the only real evidence anyone has on the question. Everything else is reasoning from a single example, which is why you should be careful with confident answers, including your own. Good scientists treat each new result as a nudge to the odds, not as a verdict on the whole question. That humility is the only honest way to approach a question this large.

So is alien life our doom? Only if the hard gate is ahead of us. And for now, nobody can say where that gate is, or whether we have already walked through it.
