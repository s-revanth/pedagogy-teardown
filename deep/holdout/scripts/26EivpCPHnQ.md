## 2. Full narration script

[SPONSOR, about 40 s, admin]

Before we start, a quick word from today's sponsor. Imagine a world where you do not have to remember three different passwords for the thing that stores your other passwords. That is the feeling of a service that tidies up your life, and I am told it is very good. I am not going to pretend I understand the technical side, but I did try it, and nothing exploded, which is a better record than most of the people in today's video. There is a link in the description, and now, back to the carnage.

[HOOK]

Alright, picture a family group chat with a few hundred relatives. The old boss has just died, and every single cousin has typed the same message: "I am the rightful leader now." Nobody has a plan. Half of them have private armies. That is basically the story of China's most famous era of warfare, the Three Kingdoms period.

Now, some of you may know it from the video games, where one guy slices through a thousand soldiers at once. Today we are going to see the history that sits behind the game, and I will be honest, the real history is a lot less tidy and a lot more embarrassing.

[BASELINE AND FLIP]

It starts with the Han dynasty. And on paper, the Han are doing amazing. They have been in charge for about four hundred years. They invented paper. They have a huge empire, a big trade road that brings in silk money, and a government with a proper exam-ish system for finding smart officials. If you were looking at China around the year 100, you would say, wow, this is a stable place. Nothing could possibly go wrong.

Right? Wrong. Very, very wrong.

Because by the year 180, the Han dynasty is basically a house with a beautiful front door and no floor inside. So, to understand how a country this big falls apart, we only need to understand one problem, and I am going to show it to you twice, so that by the third time you can say it with me.

[ROADMAP]

Here is the whole thing in one chain. Emperors keep being kids. Kids need babysitters. The babysitters turn out to be corrupt, so the emperor swaps them for a different set of babysitters, who are also corrupt. The country gets so miserable that the farmers revolt. The government can only respond by letting local commanders raise their own armies. And those commanders say, thank you very much, and keep the armies. Boom. Warlords. Then those warlords fight until only three are left. That is it. That is the video. I will just add some details and a lot of swords.

[CAVEAT]

One note before the details. Much of what people think they know about this era comes from a very famous historical novel, written over a thousand years later, which turns real guys into superheroes and villains. I will flag when I am using the novel instead of the actual records, because otherwise the history teachers in the comments will find me.

[TEMPLATE, ROUND ONE]

Okay. Here is the problem. Let's say the emperor of China dies at a young age, as emperors often did, and the new emperor is a nine-year-old. A nine-year-old cannot run a country, so who does? His mother, and since her family is much bigger than she is, the family comes along too.

Mother's family: "Don't worry, kid, we will handle the empire. You just play with your toys."

Emperor: "Okay!"

And for a while, that is fine. But the kid grows up, and he looks around and realizes that every decision about his empire has been made by his mom's uncle. He wants power back, and he cannot trust the in-laws, because they have the army. So who is left? Well, the palace is full of servants, called eunuchs, who live with the emperor, know him personally, and can get close to him in a way no general can.

Emperor: "Hey, you guys, will you help me get rid of my in-laws?"

Eunuchs: "Absolutely, Your Majesty. We would love nothing more."

They do. The in-laws are out. The emperor feels very clever. And the eunuchs, now that they have all the power, start doing what everyone with power does: they take bribes, they take land, and they put their cousins in charge of the provinces.

[TEMPLATE, ROUND TWO]

And now the emperor dies, and the next emperor is, guess what, a child.

So we have a kid on the throne. Whose family steps in? The mother's family.

In-laws: "Do not worry, kid..."

Wait, I am doing the same joke. Because that is literally what happened again. And the new emperor grows up, wants his power back, can only trust the eunuchs, and uses them to wipe out the in-laws. And the eunuchs take everything. Same song, different emperor. This was how the Han dynasty ran, twice over, and each time the court got a little more rotten.

[EMPEROR LING]

By the time we get to Emperor Ling, the system has taken a turn for the stupid. Ling has found a very efficient way to make money. He sells jobs. If you want to be a governor of a province, there is a price. Want a better province? Pay more. It is basically a pay-to-win game, where the prize is the right to rob a few million people.

Official: "I paid a lot for this job, so I need to make my money back."

Farmer: "But I have no money."

Official: "Then I will take your harvest."

Then there are floods, and then there is a famine, and the officials still want their taxes. People are starving and furious. Which brings us to the first big explosion.

[YELLOW TURBANS]

A preacher named Zhang Jue starts telling people that the heavens are about to hand the world to a new, kinder age, and that they can join it by wearing a yellow headband. And to a farmer who is being robbed by a man who bought his job, this sounds fantastic. Hundreds of thousands of followers show up. They are called the Yellow Turbans, because once again the naming department took the day off.

And now the government has a problem. The imperial army is small, corrupt, and far away. So the emperor says the thing that sets up the rest of the story: "Local governors, please raise your own armies and deal with this."

Take a second to appreciate that sentence. The central government has just handed weapons to every ambitious person in the country. This is like a landlord giving every tenant a spare key and saying "please do not be tempted."

[THREE HEROES]

Let me introduce three guys, and I am only going to give each of them one trait, because otherwise this gets confusing.

Cao Cao. He wants to be in charge, ideally of everything, and he is smart and ruthless about it.

Liu Bei. He wants to restore the Han dynasty, and he has a cool family name that might make him a distant relative of the old emperors. He has no money, no land, and a great deal of charisma.

And Sun Jian, and later his son Sun Quan, in the south. They want their own territory, and they would very much like to be left alone.

Now, here is the part from the novel, and I am flagging it: Liu Bei meets two big guys, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, and they decide to become sworn brothers in a peach garden.

Liu Bei: "I will be your brother."

Guan Yu: "I will die for you."

Zhang Fei: "I will also die for you, and I am also very loud."

Historians say this is probably not exactly how it happened, but the three of them really were extremely close, so I am going to let it slide.

[HE JIN AND DONG ZHUO: ROUND THREE]

Okay, back to the capital. Emperor Ling dies. He has two sons, and a general named He Jin, who is the older son's uncle, gets involved. We are back to the babysitters again, but this time there is a twist: He Jin wants to kill all the eunuchs once and for all, and he cannot manage it alone. So he does the thing that people always do in this story. He phones a stronger guy.

He Jin: "Hey, Dong Zhuo, could you bring your army to the capital and help me scare the eunuchs?"

Dong Zhuo: "Sure."

And I would like you to notice the pattern: a weak person invites a strong person to help with a problem, and the strong person says yes. It is the same mistake, third time, with a different haircut. The eunuchs kill He Jin. His supporters kill the eunuchs. And Dong Zhuo arrives, in the middle of all the chaos, to find a child emperor wandering around with nobody guarding him.

Dong Zhuo: "Oh, that is convenient."

He grabs the emperor. Remember this detail, because a living, breathing emperor is the most valuable object in China, and everyone is going to want to hold him.

[DONG ZHUO]

Dong Zhuo turns out to be exactly as bad as you are imagining. He kills officials he does not like. He replaces the emperor with a different child who is easier to control. He lets his soldiers run through towns, and eventually he moves the whole capital and burns the old one.

This is too much even for the warlords, so a coalition forms to take him down, and for a minute it looks great: lots of armies, lots of flags, very impressive. Then comes the actual fighting part.

Warlord one: "You go first."

Warlord two: "No, you go first."

Warlord three: "I am making lunch."

Nobody wants to lose their own army fighting Dong Zhuo, because then somebody else will be stronger. So the coalition basically becomes a very large, very armed picnic.

[LU BU]

What kills Dong Zhuo is not an army. It is his own bodyguard, Lu Bu, the best fighter in the country. And now, again, the novel: a minister, Wang Yun, introduces a beautiful girl called Diaochan to both of them, and she makes each of them think she likes him best.

Dong Zhuo: "She is mine."

Lu Bu: "She is mine."

Diaochan: "Both of you are so strong."

The real history says there was a quarrel and a woman may have been involved, but the "plan" is probably the novel's addition. Either way, Lu Bu kills him, and the people of the capital celebrate. And then Lu Bu, you will not be surprised to hear, turns out to also be a problem.

[CAO CAO WINS THE EMPEROR]

Now here is the pay-off of the detail I told you to remember. The emperor has been wandering around, with nobody looking after him, and Cao Cao realizes something brilliant. If you hold the emperor, then everything you do is, technically, on behalf of the government. Your enemies are not just rivals, they are rebels.

Cao Cao: "Your Majesty, would you like a place to stay, and perhaps a warm meal?"

Emperor: "Yes, please."

Cao Cao: "Wonderful. Everything I say is now your will."

It is a very clean piece of manipulation. It is like becoming the most powerful person in a company by picking up the CEO's phone.

[GUANDU]

His biggest rival is Yuan Shao, who has more land, more soldiers, and a far better family name. At the Battle of Guandu, Yuan Shao has something like several times as many men. Cao Cao has fewer men, but he has a plan, and the plan is lunch. He sneaks around, finds where the enemy keeps its food supplies, and sets them on fire.

An army that cannot eat is not an army. Yuan Shao's soldiers panic, and Cao Cao wins. So the north of China is now his. The pattern, if you are keeping score: whoever controls the emperor and the food usually wins.

[LIU BEI AND ZHUGE LIANG]

Meanwhile, Liu Bei is wandering from one warlord to another, losing battles, and never staying in one place. He has the charm and the loyal brothers, but nothing else. Then someone tells him about a genius living quietly in the countryside, a man called Zhuge Liang.

So Liu Bei goes to his house. He knocks. A servant says Zhuge Liang is out. Liu Bei goes home. He tries again, and again the guy is out. He goes a third time, and this time, in the novel version, he waits outside while the genius takes a nap.

Liu Bei: "I will wait."

Zhuge Liang, on waking: "Oh, you are still here?"

Honestly, I think anyone who does that to a recruiter should be hired immediately. Zhuge Liang agrees, and his advice is: Cao Cao is too strong in the north to attack, so team up with Sun Quan in the south and make a triangle.

[RED CLIFFS]

And that brings us to the most famous battle of the era: Red Cliffs. Cao Cao now has the north, and he wants the south. He brings around two hundred thousand men, or at least that is what he told everyone, which is a good number for scaring the enemy. He has a big navy, but many of his northern soldiers get seasick, so he chains his ships together so that the boats do not rock.

Remember that, because that sentence is the setup for something bad.

Liu Bei and Sun Quan have far fewer men, but their commander notes that a fleet of ships tied together, made of wood, in a strong wind, is basically one giant firework. So they send fire ships toward the enemy. The wind blows the right way. The whole fleet goes up in flames.

Cao Cao: "I had a very good plan."

Sun Quan's commander: "And we had matches."

Cao Cao retreats. And the result is that nobody is going to conquer everything for a while.

[THREE KINGDOMS]

So China settles into three states. Cao Cao's son takes the throne from the last Han emperor and creates the state of Wei in the north. Liu Bei declares himself emperor of Shu in the southwest, saying he is the continuation of the Han. And Sun Quan eventually takes the title of emperor of Wu in the southeast. Three emperors, three kingdoms, one country that used to have a single one.

Liu Bei dies after a disastrous revenge attack, and Zhuge Liang spends the rest of his life leading Shu on expeditions against Wei, which he never manages to finish. And then the generations roll on, and the heroes are replaced by their kids and their ministers.

[TONAL PIVOT]

Now, a serious moment. Every skit I have shown you hides a big problem: this was one of the deadliest periods in Chinese history. The population fell dramatically, by a large share by some estimates, though the numbers are not reliable. These were not little fights between heroes. They were wars that destroyed towns, wrecked farms, and left millions of ordinary people with nothing. We can joke about the generals. We cannot really joke about the famine.

[ENDING]

So who wins? Not Cao Cao, not Liu Bei, not Sun Quan. The winner turns out to be a man named Sima Yi, an advisor in Wei, whose family slowly takes over the government, in a move that looks suspiciously like the in-laws and eunuchs of the beginning. His grandson founds the Jin dynasty, conquers Shu, then Wu, and reunifies the country.

So after all of this, a hundred years of heroes, all the oaths and the fire ships and the strategists and the poems, the person who ends up in charge is the quiet guy at the back, with a very good understanding of who to wait out.

Which brings us back to our big group chat. The old boss dies, the cousins all claim the title, and one of them sends: "Maybe let's invite a really strong guy to help us sort this out." I think we all know how that ends.

That is the Three Kingdoms. If you liked this, there is a link to my patreon and a next video coming soon, and I will see you in the next one.
