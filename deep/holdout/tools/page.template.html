New gaps found in round 4

  • Tie-break bug. SebastianLague's training hooks tied three ways and the card picked story-anecdote. Real test hooks are 86% premise-claim, and v3 scored 14% there.
  • Sentence length is partly a caption artifact. Lague, Veritasium and Primer have barely punctuated captions (p90 of 75, 34 and 36 words). 3Blue1Brown's 24-word median is real.
  • Card conflict. Some cards set teaser-next as the ending while the teaser switch is OFF. Agents wrote teasers that dodge the detector.
  • Goodhart effects. Agents padded sentences with semicolons and two read the scoring code. The in-band score (83%) is partly gamed. The fidelity gain against the real transcript is not.
  • Corpus contamination. One CGPGrey exemplar contains another narrator.

Round 5

  1. Fix the tie-break, the answer-upfront definition and the switch/ending conflict.
  2. Train a title → hook/ending classifier on the 106 training labels, and use it only if it beats the majority under leave-one-out.
  3. Add topic to the neighbour search, since number and example rates follow the topic.
  4. Trim unpunctuated captions and filter other speakers out of the corpus.
  5. Add a content metric, such as beat-sequence distance on a labelled sample, so the score isn't style alone.

Caveats: v3 bundles six changes with no ablation. All hook and ending labels come from one annotator. Training labels were mapped from round-1 analyses. v3 narrations are longer (2,200-word cap against 1,800). Details: deep/round3/RESULTS.md.

Rule gap map: what's missing

  • Self-reports can't be trusted. 74 numeric-rule "applied" claims fail measurement. The teaser default fails 15 times and the roadmap default 14.
  • Sentence length complies 46% of the time. Every condition writes sentences too short (−0.5 to −0.7 IQR).
  • Number rate complies 46%. v2 overshoots on dates and figures. Hedges 57%, "I/me" 56%.
  • Bands describe the channel, not the video. Only 48% of real test values fall in p25–p75.
  • U, C and D rules can't be measured. They're "applied" on the generator's word alone and were almost never marked broken.
  • 3Blue1Brown's bands mix formats. v2 drifts out of band (15%) because lessons and long proofs share one target.
  • Length versus pace. The 1,800-word cap makes the over-10-minute wpm rule impossible to meet.

Round 4: how to climb

  1. Add per-channel hook and ending base rates as switches. Stop defaulting to question hooks and callback endings.
  2. Condition the numeric targets on format class: lesson, proof, devlog, mini-war, explainer.
  3. Give every U, C and D rule a measurable test, or drop it.
  4. Run features.py inside generation so "applied" is verified, not claimed.
  5. Replace the prose report with rules. v1 shows prose adds nothing.
  6. Lift the word cap for long videos.

Caveats: base read the round-1 lens in BRIEF.md. The rulebooks were built before the caption refit. v2 mixes rules and exemplars. Primer, Reducible and Lague have 8, 5 and 8 videos. The slot labels are one blind annotator. Features measure style, not explanation quality. Full write-up: deep/round3/RESULTS.md.

Round 2 (below): the first held-out test, rated by Opus
Methodology

How the test was run

We treated each channel report as a trained model and ran it on a held-out test set. A writer who never saw the test video tried to produce its script from the hypothesis alone. A separate judge then compared that script with the real transcript. Where the prediction failed, that's a gap in the hypothesis.

Training set · round 1 + deep

1. Hypothesis

12 analysed videos per channel became a report and a storyboard HTML.

reports/<ch>.md · storyboards/<ch>.html
Test split · 2 per channel

2. Held-out pick

From each channel's full video list we took the most-viewed videos (5-40 min) that weren't in tracker.csv or round 1. One of each pair was a typical video and one a different format.

3. Blind generator

It read only the report, the round-1 findings and the taxonomy, plus the title and duration. It wrote a beat sheet, the full narration, a rule trace and falsifiable predictions.

4. Critic

It read the real transcript and wrote the real beat sheet. It scored the predictions as hit, near or miss, aligned the arcs, sorted each gap by type and proposed rule edits.

Opus 5.5 · medium · ×1

5. Rater

It read every gap file, spot-checked critic claims against the transcripts, recounted wpm for all 18 videos, rated each channel and merged the findings into universal rules.

holdout/RATING.md · visual_data.json

Exactly how each step worked

  1. Transcripts. The 18 test videos were fetched through the local youtube-downloader API at 127.0.0.1:8765 with timestamps. They were compacted into lines of about 15 s with the same compact.py logic used for training (holdout/tools/fetch_holdout.py).
  2. Blind generation. One generator per channel, following GEN_BRIEF.md. Transcript folders and the web were off-limits, and the generator had to declare its recall risk for each video (none, vague or strong).
  3. Predictions. Each script commits to fixed slots: first question time, first notation time, misconception, brute force, example count and how examples grow, hook type, ending type, and the top 5 moves.
  4. Critique. One critic per channel, following CRITIC_BRIEF.md, and only after its generator had finished. Gap types: missing-rule, wrong-rule, underspecified, topic-conditional, generator-error (not charged to the hypothesis) and voice/craft. Six 1-5 scores per video.
  5. Rating. Opus at medium effort, following RATER_BRIEF.md. It was told to downgrade hits that looked like recall or general subject knowledge, and to verify critic claims, including the wpm figures, against the raw transcripts.

Leakage controls and known weaknesses

  • Recall leakage. Generators flagged some recall for 3 videos (the horses video, the LLM overview and the Basel lamp idea). Critics discounted those hits, so the adjusted hit rate falls from about 55% to about 45%.
  • Topic-guess errors. 2 topics were guessed wrong from the title alone (Primer's apology video and Veritasium's electron-microscope video). These were scored as generator errors.
  • Subject-knowledge confound. History chronology and algorithm order give some arc hits for free, regardless of the report.
  • Small sample. Only 2 videos per channel, one critic per video, and no baseline without a report. The test was also skewed off-format on purpose: 8 of the 18 videos use a format the training set lacked.
  • Round 3 fix. 5 held-out videos per channel, a no-report baseline, a recall probe before generating, 2 critics per video with kappa agreement, and a 0-100 rubric (arc order τ 20, timing 15, move F1 25, slots 20, parameters 10, voice 10).
