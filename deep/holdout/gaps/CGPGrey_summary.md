## Recurring gaps
1. **Opening form.** Both open with a plain premise or question (0:03; 0:00), not a second-person situation. The report lists hook-question and second-person-role with no rule for choosing. In both cases the title already states the thesis.
2. **No rule list.** Neither installs "2-5 named rules" in a minute. One uses a paired coinage (mechanical muscles and minds, 0:20), the other a dice gag (0:47). Stage 3 looks fitted to rule-system topics (rulers, flags, jury).
3. **Absent scaffolding.** Toy world, brute-force-first, fake ending, rule-zero compression and a labelled speculation fence are missing from both; the generator over-applied them.
4. **Density.** Real beats run about 21 s, speech about 180-190 wpm. The generator wrote 50-70-s beats and about half the words.
5. **Unrecorded structures.** Title cards, a recurring quantified gag, ambiguity as two parses, skip-lists with meta-jokes, applied endings.

## Strongest proposed rules, ranked
1. Replace "2-5 rules in 70 s" with "one cause plus one recurring device or paired coinage by 60 s"; use a rule list only for rule-system subjects. Repeat the cause tag at least 4 times (V1 5:16, 10:14; V2 2:09, 4:21, 8:06, 9:17).
2. Title-conditional hook: if the title states the claim, open with a plain premise or question in 3 s, add a through-line analogy by about 0:20, skip the staged misconception. Cross-channel.
3. Ladder of exemptions for thesis videos: 4-6 tiers, each opened with "surely X is safe?" and broken with 2-3 named instances and one number, each with a title card (V1 7:00, 9:03, 11:06). Replaces the single fake ending. Cross-channel.
4. Reverse the analogy where the viewer's intuition is right (V1 4:08-4:24).
5. Ambiguity as two parses, then a worst case (V2 4:21, 8:26). Cross-channel for rule-text topics.
6. 15-30-s beats; end on a quantified stake or an applied game, no sponsor or teaser.

- Sec. 5.4 and 5.5: V1 asserts confidently (6:04, 12:33); V2 caveats by naming "interpretation problems", not by fencing speculation.

## Verdict
The hypothesis generalises only at the level of dispositions (replay under one cause, escalating cases, caveats, callbacks, humour all held). It does not generalise at the level of staged moves: rule lists, toy worlds, fake endings and fences came from a few training videos and do not recur. It needs topic conditionals (rule system versus evidence-stacking thesis versus legal ambiguity) and a title-carries-thesis switch. Two videos is a thin sample, and the V1 content matches were partly recall-suspect.
