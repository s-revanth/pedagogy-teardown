# Reducible held-out summary (Q_1M2JaijjQ Big O, JGQe4kiPnrU PageRank)

## Verdict
The hypothesis generalises as a move vocabulary and fails as an arc template. Of the report's content moves, almost every one that the generators called confident appeared in both real videos: failure-shown, roadmap, special-case-first, notation-after-concept, honest-caveat and recap (6 of 6 in PageRank, 5 of 6 plus a half in Big O). Strict hit rates on explicit predictions were 47% (Big O) and 44% (PageRank), about 62-63% with near credit. Overall regenerate-ability: Big O 3/5, PageRank 2/5. The report was fitted to algorithm-derivation videos (FFT, Hanoi, DFS), and both holdouts were something else: a definition-heavy concept video and a theory video.

## Recurring gaps
1. Arc selection by topic type. Neither video starts from "obvious slow approach, then failure, then fix". Big O is a story-framed measurement ladder, then a decoded definition, then a procedure and worked example. PageRank is theory-first, with the named algorithm at 77% of runtime.
2. Defaults that did not hold: misconception-first (0 of 2, imported from round-1), callback-to-hook (0 of 2), teaser (0 of 2), spectacle or paradox hooks (0 of 2). Real hooks are a classroom anecdote and an "imagine users" scenario.
3. Notation timing. "Notation last" is wrong as a position rule: the order (referent, then symbol) held, but the position ranged from 17% to 89% of runtime.
4. Voice. Self-aware reactions to jargon appear in both (Big O 0:15 and 9:31; PageRank 8:41) and are unspecified in the report.

## Strongest proposed rules, ranked
1. Add a topic-type arc selector with two new variants: property-hunt (hint-led counterexample challenges, then name the failing property; PageRank 7:46, 10:04) and definition-decode plus procedure (Big O 9:01-16:34).
2. Replace default endings and openers: no misconception beat, teaser only rarely, callback only when the hook promised a payoff, recap mirroring the roadmap order (Big O 0:44 vs 16:49), scenario or anecdote hook.
3. Re-state notation timing: symbol follows one verbal computation of its referent; name and formal definition arrive within a minute of each other, around 45-55% for definition topics, earlier for numeric-object topics.
4. Story frame with named characters when comparing two solutions (Big O 0:59-8:44).
5. Brute-force vindicated variant, with a stated approximation caveat (PageRank 13:30-19:28).
6. Hold a named application until theory is built (PageRank 19:28).
