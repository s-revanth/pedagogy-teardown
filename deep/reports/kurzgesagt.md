# Kurzgesagt: how it teaches

Sample: 12 videos (the Fermi Paradox video, sNhhvQGsMEc, had an Arabic transcript and is down-weighted; its counts and examples are used only as supporting evidence, never as the sole proof of a claim).

## 1. Bottom line
Kurzgesagt teaches by giving every abstract idea a physical, agent-like stand-in (soldiers, cruise missiles, waterfalls, laundry rooms) and then walking that stand-in through a causal story from the viewer's familiar world to the surprising conclusion. Notation is almost absent; the one hard piece of vocabulary per idea is named last, as a label for something already pictured. Pace is flat in speech and slows only through caveats, objections and a deliberately placed trap or puzzle, and every video ends by cashing in its opening hook.

## 2. Signature arc
Hook (paradox or what-if) -> scope/roadmap -> minimal definition -> analogy-driven mechanism or literal simulation -> misconception/objection -> honest caveat -> commit to an answer -> callback to the hook -> credits.

## 4. Principle scorecard (confirmed / partly / contradicted / not observed)
1. Start from the familiar: 12/0/0/0. Every video opens on a known object, fear or scene (nail wound, Earth, a 1980s computer).
2. Every symbol costs effort: 8/2/0/2. Typically one coined term per idea; Immune System (0:17-0:35) is the exception, front-loading a colour code.
3. Habit makes interfaces comfortable: 0/1/0/11. No interface to learn.
4. Analogy first, then rigor: 5/7/0/0. Analogy always first; rigor is rarely reached, only labelled.
5. Act it out: 5/5/0/2. Countdowns and "pull the trigger" (9tbxDgcv74c 1:26, JyECrGp-Sw8 2:27).
6. Brute force first: 3/2/0/7. Crude versions appear (city-by-bomb multiplication 0:35, stacked Jupiters 0:35).
7. Discovery by visible repetition: 1/7/0/4. Mostly told; Egg 5:40 is the real instance.
8. Pace by novelty: 0/11/1/0. Speech flat in every transcript; slowdown comes from caveats and puzzle beats (0FH9cgRhQ-k 4:01). Immune System contradicts: idea rate stays high throughout.
9. Repeat across examples: 5/6/1/0. Template repeats (ladders, two countdowns); analogies themselves are often used once (Information Paradox contradicts).
10. Tiny to bigger: 6/4/2/0. Contradicted by Immune System (full system first) and Marianas (starts at Tsar Bomba scale).
11. Dependency order: 11/1/0/0. Strongest principle after 1.
12. Connect to outside sources: 3/7/0/2. Real events (Krakatoa, Tsar Bomba, 2016 phage trial); sources deferred to description.
13. 12-year-old simple, expert depth: 2/10/0/0. Simple language, but tool choice is rarely justified aloud.

## 5. New principles / theories
1. **Agency before mechanism.** Give every molecule or physical process a goal (hunt, archive, hijack) and learners retain sequence without notation. Evidence: CRISPR 4:19 heist, Phage 1:37 cruise missile, Coronavirus 2:03 soldiers. Falsifiable: if the same mechanism is taught with passive-voice process description, delayed recall of step order drops.
2. **Name the paradox only after the learner has built it.** Numbers or facts accumulate first, the contradiction is named late, so it feels self-derived. Evidence: Fermi 3:15 (down-weighted), Information Paradox 4:15 after 0:29-3:19 setup, Largest Star 4:31. Falsifiable: stating the paradox at 0:00 and the evidence after yields lower engagement and no better comprehension.
3. **Plant a trap at peak pattern strength.** After a repeated template is established, break it with a counterexample. Evidence: Largest Star 4:31 (mass does not equal size), Largest Black Hole 6:29 misconception and 4:01 gap. Falsifiable: a rule corrected right after it has been confirmed several times is recalled with its exception more often than one corrected earlier.
4. **Retire fears with benchmarks, not theory.** List the viewer's fears, simulate, then return to each with a real-world measuring stick. Evidence: Marianas 3:52, All Bombs 1:32 (Krakatoa, TNT). Falsifiable: corrections framed by a known event shift belief more than equivalent first-principles explanations.
5. **Stakes before advice.** Build the causal chain, then place the action as its last rung. Evidence: Coronavirus 5:15 to 6:47, CRISPR 14:50 confrontation. Falsifiable: advice-first ordering lowers stated willingness to act.
6. **Escalate with an identical template.** Repeat the same effect-sequence at larger magnitude so the viewer feels the threshold. Evidence: All Bombs 2:27 then 4:40; Largest Black Hole rungs 2:46-8:50. Falsifiable: varying the template per escalation reduces threshold estimation accuracy.

## 6. What this channel does badly or never does
- No interfaces, notation practice, or habit-building (principle 3 unobserved 11/12).
- No inline quizzes, no prediction pauses, no learner response; discovery is staged by the narrator, with the Egg the only real instance.
- Analogies are rarely reused, so transfer is untested (Information Paradox).
- Tool choice almost never justified; rigor deferred to descriptions or follow-up videos.
- Immune System shows complexity first and ends by admitting it simplified; no scaffolding of the 21-cell map.
- Sponsor and credit beats occupy 8 of 12 endings.

## 7. Velocity translation
- **Scene builder:** a reusable "ladder" scene (fixed sentence template plus a familiar ruler) and a "countdown" scene that replays at a larger magnitude with the same layout.
- **Voice/beat script:** keep wpm flat; slow via caveat beats, voiced objections ("how could that help?" YI3tsmFsrOg 4:01) and one late term per idea.
- **Orchestrator rules:** name the paradox only after the data beats; place a trap beat after the third confirming rung; schedule a hook-callback in the last beat.
- **Playbooks:** "what-if escalation", "scale ladder with trap", "option menu then pick" (Information Paradox 4:33).
- **Learner model:** track which analogies a learner has accepted; reuse one across concepts, which Kurzgesagt rarely does.
- **Ghost hands:** point at the agent, not the symbol; the thing doing the action is what to highlight.
- **Evals:** test whether a late-named term beats an early-named one on recall; test benchmark-based correction against theory-based correction.
