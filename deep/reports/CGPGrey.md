# CGP Grey: how he teaches (12 videos)

Source: 12 manual-caption analyses (ids in `tracker.csv`), the lens in `BRIEF.md`, and round-1 findings. Quotes are limited to those already recorded in the analyses. Timestamps are m:ss. Note: two analyses use different titles than the tracker (see section 6 and the table).

## 1. Bottom line
Grey teaches by installing a tiny model, usually two to five named rules or one hidden cause, within the first minute, then replaying that same model across a ladder of settings until the viewer predicts the next case before he shows it. He casts the viewer as the actor ("you, the ruler", "you, aspiring pope"), states the wrong belief the viewer holds, and lets them commit to it before breaking it. He keeps speech fast and flat and slows only the idea rate, with the long beats going to the surprising or messy cases, and he fences off speculation and caveats so his confident tone never leaks into claims he cannot support.

## 2. Signature arc
Eight stages, seen in most of the 12 videos (the shorter videos fold stages together).

1. **Hook as a situation or paradox, second person.** Examples: the red light you keep missing (iHzzSao6ypE 0:00), "you can never become pope" (kF8I_r9XT7A 0:00), the ruler paradox (rStL7niR7gs 0:00-0:15), disqualifying jury duty (uqH_Y1TupoQ 0:00).
2. **State the expected belief, then break it.** Straight-looking border is 900 zig-zag segments (qMkYlIA7mgw 0:33); intuitive back-to-front boarding is bad (oAHbLRjF0vo 0:31); you can run for pope like for president (kF8I_r9XT7A 0:17).
3. **Minimal model or definition.** Keys and three rules (rStL7niR7gs 0:47-1:52); two brain rules (wfYbgdo8e-8 0:38); five flag rules (l4w6808wJcU 0:15); two jury rules (uqH_Y1TupoQ 0:57); colored regions (rNu8XDBSn10 0:18).
4. **Toy world or staged special case.** One-lane road with a chicken (iHzzSao6ypE 1:06); one-by-one boarding (oAHbLRjF0vo 2:10); the simplest regime, a dictator with a dozen keys (rStL7niR7gs 1:21).
5. **Replay across settings (visible repetition).** Rules rerun on democracy, resource states and corporations (rStL7niR7gs 6:11, 7:45, 8:51, 12:26); 50 flags graded against the same rubric (l4w6808wJcU 1:42-17:50); ten domains of hexagons (thOifuHs6eY 2:37-8:22).
6. **Failure, caveat or fake ending.** Fake "drive better" ending (iHzzSao6ypE 3:00, rejected 3:19); toy-demo disclaimer (oAHbLRjF0vo 4:25); "Speculation time" fence (wfYbgdo8e-8 3:07).
7. **Compression.** Alliterative 4-F checklist (wOmjnioNulo 5:11); one Venn diagram (rNu8XDBSn10 4:43); numeric funnel (kF8I_r9XT7A 4:27); numbered rules plus a zeroth rule (rStL7niR7gs 17:37).
8. **Callback to the hook, punchline, then teaser or sponsor.** Back to the opening intersection (iHzzSao6ypE 4:07); "you is two" (wfYbgdo8e-8 4:18); Utah via the bestagon video (l4w6808wJcU 17:50).

Pacing note: the first symbol is absent in 5 of 12 videos (iHzzSao6ypE, qMkYlIA7mgw, kF8I_r9XT7A, wOmjnioNulo, wfYbgdo8e-8) or is a rule list or tree on screen within the first 20-60 seconds; the one real chart arrives at 10:29 in the 18-minute video (rStL7niR7gs).

## 5. New principles and theories
1. **One cause, renamed as it grows.** Fix a single named cause early and end every new scene on "same cause again", giving the metaphor a more vivid name whenever the concept gains a property (phantom intersection, then snake, then rate rule: iHzzSao6ypE 1:25, 1:40, 1:55). Same mechanism in qMkYlIA7mgw (straight lines on unknown land explain each oddity), oAHbLRjF0vo (bag stowing) and rStL7niR7gs. Falsifiable: if a lesson ends each example on the same named cause, learners predict the next example's outcome above chance; with a new explanation per example, they do not.
2. **Bracket reality with worst case and ideal.** Design the worst case on purpose, then the ideal, then explain the real compromise (oAHbLRjF0vo 2:10, 5:19-7:24). The failed-fix ladder does the same job in history (jNgP6d9HraI 1:40, 3:45, 5:07). Falsifiable: if learners see the worst and ideal cases first, they explain why the real design is a compromise better than learners who see only the real one.
3. **Rubric, then grade.** Install 2-5 rules in about 70 seconds, then run many cases against them, ordering by narrative contrast (failure runs broken by exemplars) not difficulty (l4w6808wJcU 0:15-1:06, 4:39, 11:26; uqH_Y1TupoQ 0:57; wOmjnioNulo near-twin contrast pairs 2:40). Falsifiable: if the tutor asks for the verdict after about five cases, learners grade correctly without the rule display by case ten.
4. **Fake ending, commit, then overturn.** Let the viewer lock in the obvious answer and then reject it (iHzzSao6ypE 3:00-3:19; oAHbLRjF0vo 0:31, 4:25; qMkYlIA7mgw 0:33). Falsifiable: if learners commit before the correction, delayed retest shows fewer returns to the original belief than with a plain explanation.
5. **Speculation fence.** Label the boundary between established and guessed material with an explicit marker (wfYbgdo8e-8 3:07, 3:58; jNgP6d9HraI 2:17; l4w6808wJcU 11:57). It lets the tone stay confident without overclaiming. Falsifiable: if the tutor marks guesses, learners rate trust higher on later claims than with unmarked hedges.
6. **Hidden-structure counting reveal.** After two guided reveals, count the hidden structure aloud (thOifuHs6eY 1:35, 2:37); viewers begin finding it on their own. A persistent visual key with a final composite does related work (rNu8XDBSn10 4:43, jNgP6d9HraI tree). Falsifiable: after two guided reveals, a third unguided one succeeds more often than a third guided one.

## 6. What this channel does badly or never does
- No learner checks: no pauses, no questions that wait. Commit-to-answer is rhetorical (6 videos), never an actual wait.
- Little rigor: analogy rarely hardens into math or a proof (principle 4: 1 of 12 confirmed). The hexagon proof is thin by the analysis's own account.
- Escalation is by weirdness, not size; a strict tiny-to-big ladder is rare (principle 10).
- No outside sources used as subproblems (principle 12).
- Fast, dense: no breathing room for a learner who falls behind. The slow beats are in the content, not in the delivery.
- Several lists are meant to be skimmed (rNu8XDBSn10 3:20, 4:26), which a tutor must not copy for novices.

## 7. Velocity translation
- **Scene builder:** a car-by-car chain sim (iHzzSao6ypE); queue sim with scoring overlay (oAHbLRjF0vo); funnel primitive whose stage widths shrink with spoken counts (kF8I_r9XT7A); checklist grid of cases by criteria that ticks per case (wOmjnioNulo, l4w6808wJcU); two-pane split stage with separate visibility masks (wfYbgdo8e-8); persistent tree or graph that prunes visibly (jNgP6d9HraI); fixed color per term with a final composite (rNu8XDBSn10); "hidden structure reveal" primitive that highlights neighbours with a count.
- **Voice and beat script:** keep 160-180 wpm. Name the cause in the first 60 seconds and end each beat with "same cause again". Restate the minimal rules right before each test beat. Use a fence phrase for speculation.
- **Orchestrator rules and playbooks:** "model then replay" (teach three rules once, then cycle domains asking for predictions); "bracket it" (worst, random, ideal, compromise); "failed-fix ladder"; "rubric-then-grade" (learner verdict first after about five cases); "emergent phenomenon" (state two rules, ask what follows); fixed per-stage template for any pipeline. Use a fake-ending beat only after the learner has committed to an answer.
- **Learner model:** track unprompted application of the model in a new domain as the mastery signal (rStL7niR7gs); diagnose which classification axes a confused learner mixes and teach the axis first (rNu8XDBSn10).
- **Ghost hands:** after two guided reveals, pause and let the learner find the third; ghost hands only on timeout.
- **Evals:** analogy names change only when the concept gains a property; simplifications are flagged before a later node contradicts them; every example ends on the named cause.
