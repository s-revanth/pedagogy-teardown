# Primer: how the blob channel teaches

Data note: 12 videos, captions manual for all. Principle 8 (pacing) cannot be tested from the transcripts, because they carry no speech-rate data.

## 1. Bottom line
Primer teaches by building a small, rule-bound blob world and running it before any theory appears. The viewer predicts, the simulation settles the question, and only then do names, tables and equations show up as compressed descriptions of what was already watched. Honesty is the connective tissue: the presenter flags choices, shows his own wrong guesses, and ends on a parameter change that breaks the model, so trust comes from visible limits.

## 2. Signature arc
1. **Familiar world and one-line question.** Blobs, food, survive and reproduce, carried over from earlier videos (Aggression 0:05, Natural Selection 0:33). Non-biology videos open on a story instead: rockets and haggling (Supply and Demand 0:15), a city of rooms (Epidemic 0:15), two-party politics (Voting 0:00).
2. **Dumbest version first, as a baseline.** No mutation (Natural Selection 1:05), no altruism (Green Beard 0:49), no aging (Aging 4:00), one buyer and one seller (Supply and Demand 3:48).
3. **Add one thing at a time with a rule list.** One actor, one trait or one knob per step. Examples: add Hawk next to Dove (Aggression 1:34); 1x1, 2x1, 2x3, 3x3 markets (Supply and Demand 0:48-6:36).
4. **Predict, then run.** A pause with the outcomes listed (Natural Selection 2:14, Aggression 2:25, Green Beard 4:29, 6:38, 7:29).
5. **Surprise, then the lesson.** The sim contradicts the obvious guess. The presenter is sometimes surprised too (Natural Selection 6:34, 7:08; Rock Paper Scissors 12:51; Epidemic 10:33).
6. **Notation after concept.** A payoff table at 4:36 (Aggression), a ternary plot at 3:01 (Rock Paper Scissors), R0 at 1:24 (Epidemic). Each is introduced as a patch for a visible failure.
7. **Same machinery, new number.** Repeat the template across settings (Aggression 7:55 and 8:44; the 30-run batches in Green Beard 4:12-8:01).
8. **Build and verify.** Math or theory predicts, and a large sim confirms (Aggression 10:05, Sacrificing for Family 95%-death variant).
9. **Break the model on purpose.** A parameter flip or a caveat cashed in (Aggression 11:47 prisoner's dilemma; Cheater 18:54 staged trap; Green Beard 2:25 caveat cashed at 9:22).
10. **Teaser to the next video, then admin.** Present in 6 of 12 videos.

Variants: Logistic Growth (433 s) skips most of this and derives a term by working backward from a target curve, leaning on earlier videos. Catch a Cheater swaps the sim for a coin game and a statistical test the viewer designs.

## 5. New theories
1. **Compression-after-experience.** Formal notation is cheapest when it summarises something the learner has already watched. Evidence: payoff table after the sim (Aggression 4:36), curves after seven minutes of haggling (Supply and Demand 7:24), ternary plot after the bar graph fails (RPS 2:45). *Falsifiable:* if notation is shown before the matching sim, learners will need more replays and recall the symbols less well than a notation-after group.
2. **Costs make predictions real.** A prediction prompt only teaches when the answer is not guessable, so each benefit ships with a cost. Evidence: speed, size and sense each cost something (Natural Selection 2:14, 4:25, 5:36); foraging cases are chosen so the obvious guess is wrong (Foraging 8:38, 9:24). *Falsifiable:* if a predict pause has a guessable answer, learners will skip it or show no gain from it, compared with pauses on counterintuitive cases.
3. **The failure names the next ingredient.** Each model's break shows exactly what is missing, so the next symbol or mechanism needs no sales pitch. Evidence: Epidemic (R0 fails, S appears; the epidemic ends, lifespans appear); Aging (selection-only model, then mutation, then pleiotropy); Voting (each fix has a flaw). *Falsifiable:* if new terms follow a visible breakdown, learners will report them as needed more than terms introduced in a fixed syllabus order.
4. **Dignify, then dismiss the human intuition.** Raise the clever human move and say which model assumption rules it out. Evidence: retaliation and reward ruled out because blobs forget (Aggression 5:28, 6:03); the honest-voting intuition in IRV (Voting 5:43). *Falsifiable:* if the intuition is acknowledged, learners hold fewer residual misconceptions than if it is ignored.
5. **Planted-error challenge.** The tutor commits to a plausible wrong model with viewer buy-in, announces a trap, and lets the sim refute it. Evidence: r versus p (Sacrificing 1:38, 13:15, 14:24); the 75% assumption trap (Cheater 18:54-21:04). *Falsifiable:* learners who fell for a staged trap will state the assumption more reliably a week later than those told it directly.
6. **Honesty is a repeated, cashable move.** Caveats planted early are called back later and tuning and exaggeration are disclosed. Evidence: Green Beard 2:25 cashed at 9:22; Aging 21:41 tuning; Foraging 3:50 made-up number. *Falsifiable:* disclosed choices will be questioned less by learners than hidden ones, and trust ratings will not drop.

## 6. What Primer does badly or never does
- No analogy-first ladder: principle 4 is only partial in 8 of 12. The sim is the analogy, which assumes learners accept an abstract toy world.
- The insight is usually told after the prediction, not extracted from the learner (principle 7 partial in 4, and mostly the narrator concludes).
- No adaptation to the viewer. Pace and examples are fixed; the pause-and-rewind invitation (Foraging 6:17) is the only support.
- Dense stretches near the end of long videos pile up definitions (Aging 24:05), and Logistic Growth leans on prior videos with no catch-up.
- Tool choice is rarely justified against alternatives (principle 13 partial in 9 of 12).
- Almost never uses outside sources inside the lesson, except Aging.
- Silent sim runs of 30-40 s carry no narration, so a learner who looks away misses the result.

## 7. Velocity translation
- **Scene builder:** a sim primitive with one-knob dials, N-run batches (thin lines plus average), a live tally panel, and a fixed scenario re-runnable under different rules. Plot points as the actor acts; morph a lossy chart into a better one.
- **Voice / beat script:** state an expectation before each run and say when the run contradicts it. Name terms only after the behaviour shows. Plant a caveat early and call it back. Emit pause-and-rewind and recap beats after dense stretches.
- **Orchestrator rules:** baseline run before any variable; one change per step; predict-pause before every reveal; introduce a symbol only after a frame shows the previous one failing.
- **Playbooks:** one-knob ladder; each-fix-has-a-flaw (find the counterexample); planted-error; extreme-then-realistic with admission; work-backward-from-target-curve; scope-the-claim ending.
- **Learner model:** log each commitment and score calibration; tag numbers as choice or assumption; track which knob changed.
- **Ghost hands:** point at table cells and graph regions during narration, and walk regions in order (Logistic 3:38-4:25).
- **Evals:** check the tutor reports a case where its own expectation failed; check that predict cases have non-obvious answers.
